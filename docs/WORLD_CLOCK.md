# World clock

What year it is in this world, and what every region looked like at that moment. Every place added later takes its "now" from this file (`HISTORICAL_STANDARD.md` §1–2). Written 2026-11-18 from research lane X1 (`research/claude/X1_Claude.md`), which was checked claim by claim in `research/verification/X1.md`: 85 claims confirmed, 6 partly, none unsupported, and no invented sources. That check's corrections C1–C15 are applied here. References like `X1#34` point to a claim row in the report.

**Amended 2026-11-19** (D32, D34, D36): the world now opens in 1628 BCE, not 1615, and the viewer can move the year. A second X1 report, by Gemini Neon, was compared with this file (`research/verification/X1_Neon.md`). Its rows are cited here as X1n D1 to D14 and X1n A1 to A6.

**Grades.** Grades here follow `HISTORICAL_STANDARD.md` §3, not the lane's own scheme (verification C13).
- Every absolute calendar date is **grade B at best**: it depends on a chronology, a calibration curve or a model.
- Evidence from outside the window (1800–1500 BCE) is grade C, with its distance from the window stated.

**The world opens in 1628 BCE (D36).** The viewer can move the year (D34), so this is where the world starts, not the only year it shows. Under the high chronology the living town runs from about 1645 BCE to an end that no source fixes to a year:
- **Start:** after the rebuilding from the first earthquake, c. 1650 BCE (Rutter's high date, X1#28). Marthari's interval of two or three decades probably runs from this first earthquake.
- **End:** the earthquake whose debris was being cleared when the town was buried came **months to several years** before the eruption. Soil and plant growth on that debris suggest "a period of several years" (Manning 2022, after Doumas 1990 and Limbrey 1990, neither of which could be opened; X1n D1). The Ministry's "few tens of days", which the T7 check read as 20–90 days, may measure only the last warning.
- **The eruption:** 1613–1602 BC at 68.3% and 1618–1584 BC at 95.4% (Manning 2024, IntCal20; X1n D2).
- **INFERENCE:** the earliest the town can have stood damaged is about 1623 BCE: the 95.4% bound of 1618, less up to five years, which is the 68.3% span of the interval in Manning's model. 1628 is before that on every reading.

The first reading of the two intervals was an inference by the T7 checker (`research/verification/T7.md`), because Marthari's papers could not be opened. The comparison of 2026-11-19 corrected the second.

In 1628 the town is whole and lived in, about two decades after the rebuild, with its paintings fresh. Beds in the streets, walled-up doors and partial clearance belong to the town's last phase. They are not shown in the opening year; the year slider reaches them (D37).

**1615 BCE was the world's year on 2026-11-18 and 2026-11-19** (D16). It lies inside the 95.4% range above, which is why the year moved (D32).

"No bodies were found" holds only for the roughly 10% of the town that has been excavated.

| Low | c. 1525–1490 BC | Archaeological links to Egypt (Warren, Wiener). Theran pumice appears only in Thutmosid (early New Kingdom) contexts, which gives a latest possible date. | X1#3, #20, #21, #23 |

**Also relevant:**
- **The Ahmose temple straw:** 1542–1427 cal BCE (66.4%). The often-quoted 1545–1504 cal BCE (48.9%) is the combined mean of three mudbrick samples, not the straw alone (C1).
- **The 1628/1627 signal is not Thera.** It is Alaska's Aniakchak II eruption, in the world's opening year. The Greenland tephra of that date was already rejected as Theran by 2008 (X1#17, #79; C4).
- **Tsunami deposits:** Çeşme's seed gives 1612–1573 (19.4%) and 1565–1501 cal BCE (76.1%) at 95%: no earlier than 1612, and its authors say it also supports the low chronology (X1#78; X1n D8). Malia gives pre-event 1744–1544 and post-event 1509–1430 BCE (X1#77).

**Notes on the table:**
- **Destructions in the northern Levant:** not all of them are Hattusili I's. Hattusili's Annals record campaigns against Alalakh and its neighbours, but Ebla's excavator now attributes Ebla's destruction to Pizikarra of Nineveh. The 1632–1610 BC range (56.2%) is the date of Zincirli's destruction alone (C6).
- **The world's opening year is a fixed point.** Aniakchak II erupts in Alaska in 1628 BCE (ice cores), and trees on three continents show frost rings in 1627 BC. A cold summer is attested worldwide (X1#17, #79). How it showed in the Aegean sky, if at all, is not known.

## 6. Calendars: how "today's date" may be shown
- **Egypt:** the reigning king's regnal year, then the month, season and day of the 365-day civil calendar. Its three seasons are Akhet, Peret and Shemu, with 5 extra days at the end of the year. Twelve months of 30 days is an inference from those totals, not stated in a source we opened (X1#37, #40–#43, #90; G5).
- **Babylonia:** a year name of the reigning king, then a lunar month (Nisannu to Addaru) and its day. Days run from sunset to sunset, and the year begins near the spring equinox (X1#46, #71, #72).
- **Assyria and Kanesh:** eponym years (X1#73).
- **The Aegean, Cyprus, the Levant and Kerma:** no attested way of counting time. Show only the season and the moon's phase, never an invented month or year name (Historical Standard, rule 4; X1#75).
