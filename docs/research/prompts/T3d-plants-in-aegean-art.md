I am building a real-time 3D reconstruction of the island of Thera (Santorini) and its Bronze Age town at Akrotiri, as they stood shortly before the Late Bronze Age ("Minoan") eruption. It runs in a web browser, and everything in it is generated by code, with no photographs, textures or 3D model files. So I need concrete, sourced facts and numbers (shapes, sizes, colours, quantities) that code can be built from, and an honest grade of how sure each one is. The world shows one moment: the year 1615 BCE on the high (radiocarbon) chronology, in late May, about four weeks before the summer solstice, inside a window of about 1800–1500 BCE. Other places at the same moment will be added later, so every date must be stated in a way that can be compared across regions.

## Source rules (important)
1. Cite a source for every claim, with a link where one exists. Prefer the excavators' own publications (the Akrotiri excavation of the Archaeological Society at Athens: Marinatos, Doumas, Palyvou and the current team), peer-reviewed journals, conference volumes, museum catalogues and specialists' books. Tourist sites, popular articles and AI-written pages are not sources; use Wikipedia only to find the sources it cites.
2. Grade every claim's confidence:
   - **A:** found on Thera in a pre-eruption context (Late Cycladic I), or a scientific analysis of such material (pollen, charcoal, seeds, bone, residue, pigment, radiocarbon).
   - **B:** shown in Theran art of the time (it shows what Therans saw or imagined, not that it existed on the island); or found at a contemporary site elsewhere in the Aegean and reasonably carried over to Thera; or found on Thera but its interpretation is debated.
   - **C:** later evidence, analogy (later periods, other regions, ethnography), a modern reconstruction, or a hypothesis. A plant shown only in the art of another Aegean place is C for Thera.
3. Shown is not present. For every plant, animal, object or landscape taken from a wall painting, say whether it is also physically attested on Thera.
4. Dates: give each piece of evidence its archaeological phase (e.g. Late Cycladic I, Late Minoan IA) and an absolute date range, say whether the range follows the "high" (radiocarbon) or "low" (Egyptian-linked) chronology, and how far it is from the eruption.
5. Numbers: whenever a source gives a measurement (size, height, depth, area, count, distance), give the number, its unit and its range as the source states them. If you convert units, say "converted".
6. Never guess. If you cannot find something, write "could not verify". Label your own reasoning INFERENCE. Mark single-source claims [verify]. Linear A is undeciphered: never invent a Minoan word or name, and say plainly when no ancient term is known.
7. Read only: no sign-ups, purchases or downloads.
8. Rights: when you cite a plan, map, photograph, 3D model or dataset, state its licence or terms of use if the source states them.

## LANE T3d: plants in Aegean art, on Thera and elsewhere, and how far to trust them

The ground of the island is still bare. The project may use the art of the time to help decide how plants looked and grew together, but never to place a plant on the island by itself. This lane gathers what the art shows and what specialists say it can prove. Leads, not sources: Möbius 1933; Morgan 1988; Shaw 1993; Porter 2000; Sarpaki 2000; Chapin 2004; Day 2011. Cite one only if you open it.

### Topics
A. **Every plant in the wall paintings of Akrotiri.** Building, room and wall for each (for example the Spring Fresco of Delta 2, the crocus gatherers and the reed bed of Xeste 3, the House of the Ladies, the river landscape and the towns of the West House miniature frieze, Building Beta, Sector Alpha). For each: the plant as specialists identify it, and the debate; the colours used, and the pigments where analysed; its size on the wall; what it grows from (rock, water, soil, a pot); and what stands beside it.
B. **Plants on Akrotiri's painted pottery and other objects** of the final phase (for example barley ears, vetch, crocus, lilies and reeds on jugs, ewers and offering tables): which plants, how they are drawn, and where published.
C. **The same plants elsewhere in the Aegean in the window** (Middle Minoan III to Late Minoan IA, Late Cycladic I): for example the House of the Frescoes and the Saffron Gatherer at Knossos, the lily paintings of Amnisos, Hagia Triada, Phylakopi on Melos, Ayia Irini on Kea, Trianda on Rhodes, and Floral Style pottery. What each adds to Thera's own art: other species, gardens or planted beds, trees, cultivated fields.
D. **How literal the painters were.** What specialists conclude about convention and invention: hybrid plants that combine features of several species; plants shown flowering together that do not; colours chosen by convention; plants taken from Egyptian or Near Eastern art (papyrus, palms); and whether the painted landscapes show Thera, Crete or no real place.
E. **Shown and present.** For every plant in A–C, one line: found as physical remains on Thera; found elsewhere in the Aegean in the window; growing wild in the Cyclades today; or none of these.

### Assumptions to test
- **T3d-1:** The painted lilies, crocuses and sea daffodils of Akrotiri mix observation with convention, and some combine more than one species.
- **T3d-2:** Papyrus and date palm in Aegean art came from foreign art or travel, and neither grew on Thera.
- **T3d-3:** Akrotiri's painted pottery shows barley and pulses that are also found there as seeds.
- **T3d-4:** Aegean art of the window shows flowers on rocky ground far more often than fields, orchards or vineyards.
- **T3d-5:** No wall painting can be shown to depict a particular real place on Thera.
- **T3d-6:** The Amnisos lily paintings show plants in a made garden.
