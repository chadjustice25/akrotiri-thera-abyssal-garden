## LANE W1: the date of the Thera eruption and the Aegean phases

The project must pick one year for "now" at Thera and show every other region at that same year. The eruption's date is the anchor, and it is disputed, so I need the full current state of the evidence, not one answer.

### Topics
A. **The date of the eruption today.** The radiocarbon evidence (for example the olive branch buried on Thera, short-lived samples from Akrotiri, IntCal20 and the newer annual tree-ring calibration work), ice cores and tree rings (and whether any ice-core tephra has been chemically matched to Thera), the Çeşme-Bağlararası evidence in western Anatolia, the archaeological synchronisms (Theran pumice at Tell el-Dab'a and other Egyptian and Levantine contexts, Cypriot White Slip I at Akrotiri, links to the Egyptian 18th Dynasty) and the debate over Ahmose's "Tempest Stela". Give each current estimate with its range and its authors, and say where the "high" and "low" positions stand now.
B. **The Aegean phases.** Middle Minoan III, Late Minoan IA and IB, Late Cycladic I, and Middle and Late Helladic (including the Mycenae Shaft Graves): their absolute dates under the high and the low chronology, and how long Akrotiri's final phase lasted before the eruption, including the earthquake that came before it.
C. **How it is presented.** How current handbooks, major museums (for example the Museum of Prehistoric Thera, the British Museum, the Metropolitan Museum) and recent syntheses state the eruption date, and how they handle the high/low disagreement in public.

### Assumptions to test
- **W1-1:** The eruption happened between about 1630 and 1500 BCE: radiocarbon has favoured the later 17th century BCE, and Egyptian-linked archaeology about 1530–1500 BCE.
- **W1-2:** Recent tree-ring-based radiocarbon calibration has narrowed the gap and points to the 16th century BCE.
- **W1-3:** Akrotiri's final phase (Late Cycladic I / Late Minoan IA) falls within 1800–1500 BCE under either chronology.
- **W1-4:** Late Minoan IA lasted several decades at least, and the earthquake that damaged Akrotiri came months to a few years before the eruption.
- **W1-5:** No ice-core tephra has been securely matched to the Thera eruption.
