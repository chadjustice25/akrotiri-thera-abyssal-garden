I am building a real-time 3D reconstruction of the island of Thera (Santorini) and its Bronze Age town at Akrotiri, as they stood shortly before the Late Bronze Age ("Minoan") eruption. ## LANE T3b: what grew where on Thera, and how thickly

The ground of the island is still bare. An earlier lane established which plants are attested on Thera (cereals and pulses, olive, fig, vine, oak, pine, tamarisk and low scrub). This lane asks where they stood and how thickly.

### Topics
A. **Farmland under the pumice.** Terrace and field walls, tracks, threshing floors, cultivation marks or buried soils found beneath the Minoan tephra on Thera and Therasia (for example the burnt terrace stones with a charred olive shrub on Therasia), and the rural sites of the period. Where each is, its size, and what it shows of land use.
B. **The buried land surface.** The soil directly under the Minoan tephra: where it is exposed, its thickness and colour, and the plant remains, root casts and tree moulds in it or in the tephra above (such as the olive tree buried standing on the caldera rim). Keep older fossil floras in the island's tuffs apart, with their dates.
C. **Zones of vegetation.** What specialists reconstruct for the island just before the eruption: the shares of cultivated land, grazed scrub (phrygana, maquis), open woodland and bare rock; where trees stood (gullies, north slopes, the limestone of Profitis Ilias, the coast); and the plants of wet ground, stream beds and the shore (reeds, rushes, tamarisk). Give the charcoal, seed, weed and dung evidence behind each, for Late Cycladic I in particular.
D. **How thickly things grew.** For each kind of cover, a usable number: trees per hectare in a dry-farmed olive grove; vines per hectare and how vines were trained; the shares of arable land under cereals, pulses and fallow; shrub height and percentage cover in grazed phrygana and maquis; tree spacing in open oak or pine woodland. Prefer Bronze Age Aegean evidence (for example dated terraces on Crete, Kythera or Pseira); grade traditional Cycladic farming and modern vegetation surveys C. Say how today's Santorini misleads: its soil is the eruption's own pumice and ash.
E. **The ground itself.** Colours and textures of the pre-eruption surface where no plant covers it: soils on the older lavas and scoria, on limestone and schist; beach sand and pebbles on the south coast; bare rock. Give measured colours (Munsell or similar) where published.

### Assumptions to test
- **T3b-1:** Farm terraces already existed on Thera and Therasia before the eruption.
- **T3b-2:** A buried soil lies under the Minoan tephra across the island, and remains in it show what grew where.
- **T3b-3:** The gentler ground round Akrotiri was mostly farmed, with grazed scrub on steep or rocky ground.
- **T3b-4:** Trees were scattered (open woodland and groves), not closed forest.
- **T3b-5:** No pollen record of pre-eruption date exists from Thera itself.
- **T3b-6:** Today's low basket-trained vines of Santorini (kouloura) cannot be assumed for 1615 BCE.
