I am building a real-time 3D reconstruction of the island of Thera (Santorini) and its Bronze Age town at Akrotiri, as they stood in the last months before the Late Bronze Age ("Minoan") eruption. It runs in a web browser, and everything in it is generated by code (terrain, sea, buildings, plants, animals, boats, people and sound), with no photographs, textures or 3D model files. So I need concrete, sourced facts and numbers (shapes, sizes, materials, colours, quantities) that code can be built from, and an honest grade of how sure each one is. The project's time window is c. 1650 BCE, give or take 100–150 years (about 1800–1500 BCE). Later the same world will grow to other places at the same moment (Crete, Egypt, Byblos, Cyprus, the Levant, Anatolia, Mesopotamia), so every date must be stated in a way that can be compared across regions.

## Source rules (important)
1. Cite a source for every claim, with a link where one exists. Prefer the excavators' own publications (the Akrotiri excavation of the Archaeological Society at Athens: Marinatos, Doumas, Palyvou and the current team), peer-reviewed journals, conference volumes, museum catalogues and specialists' books. Tourist sites, popular articles and AI-written pages are not sources; use Wikipedia only to find the sources it cites.
2. Grade every claim's confidence:
   - **A:** found on Thera in a pre-eruption context (Late Cycladic I), or a scientific analysis of such material (pollen, charcoal, seeds, bone, residue, pigment, radiocarbon).
   - **B:** shown in Theran art of the time (it shows what Therans saw or imagined, not that it existed on the island); or found at a contemporary site elsewhere in the Aegean and reasonably carried over to Thera; or found on Thera but its interpretation is debated.
   - **C:** later evidence, analogy (later periods, other regions, ethnography), a modern reconstruction, or a hypothesis.
3. Shown is not present. For every plant, animal, object or landscape taken from a wall painting, say whether it is also physically attested on Thera.
4. Dates: give each piece of evidence its archaeological phase (e.g. Late Cycladic I, Late Minoan IA) and an absolute date range, say whether the range follows the "high" (radiocarbon) or "low" (Egyptian-linked) chronology, and how far it is from the eruption.
5. Numbers: whenever a source gives a measurement (size, height, depth, area, count, distance), give the number, its unit and its range as the source states them. If you convert units, say "converted".
6. Never guess. If you cannot find something, write "could not verify". Label your own reasoning INFERENCE. Mark single-source claims [verify]. Linear A is undeciphered: never invent a Minoan word or name, and say plainly when no ancient term is known.
7. Read only: no sign-ups, purchases or downloads.
8. Rights: when you cite a plan, map, photograph, 3D model or dataset, state its licence or terms of use if the source states them.

## LANE T6: the wall paintings, their colours, and the look of things

### Topics
A. **A catalogue of the wall paintings of Akrotiri** by building and room: subject, which wall and floor, size, how much is original and how much restored, and where each is now (Museum of Prehistoric Thera, National Archaeological Museum in Athens, or on site). Include at least the West House (the Fisherman, the miniature frieze, the ikria, the "priestess"), Xeste 3 (saffron gatherers, adorants), Delta 2 (the Spring Fresco), Beta 1 (boxing boys, antelopes), Beta 6 (monkeys), the House of the Ladies and Xeste 4.
B. **Technique:** true fresco and secco, the plaster layers, string and incised guidelines, the order of painting, and how the painters worked.
C. **Pigments and colours:** the pigments identified by analysis (e.g. Egyptian blue, glaucophane or riebeckite blues, haematite and ochre reds, goethite yellow, lime white, carbon black, and any green, pink or purple), where each came from, and measured colour values of pigments or painted surfaces where published (Munsell, CIELAB, reflectance spectra). How colours have faded or changed, and what restorers added.
D. **Pottery and decorated objects in the last phase:** the main local and imported wares and shapes, their decoration (spirals, reeds, lilies, ripple patterns, swallows on nippled ewers), their colours, and stone vessels.
E. **Drawing conventions** for re-creating the look in code: skin colours, background colours, the multicoloured wavy bands used for rocks, how land and sea are drawn, and the range of colours in a typical painted room.
F. **Rights:** what current Greek law (e.g. Law 4858/2021 on antiquities) says about reproducing images of antiquities such as these paintings in a free public website and in a commercial game; whether re-creating a motif in code, without using any photograph, needs permission; and the terms on which museum and Wikimedia photographs of them are published.

### Assumptions to test
- **T6-1:** Most Akrotiri wall paintings were painted at least partly in true fresco on wet lime plaster.
- **T6-2:** The palette is mostly red and yellow ochres, Egyptian blue and other blues, lime white and black, with few or no greens.
- **T6-3:** Painted rooms were mostly on upper floors.
- **T6-4:** Rocks and landscape are painted as bands of strong, varied colours, not in the island's real colours.
- **T6-5:** Reproducing these paintings in a commercial product needs permission from the Greek state, and a re-creation of a motif in code may too.
- **T6-6:** The Beta 6 monkey paintings and the West House miniature frieze both belong to the town's final phase before the eruption.

## Report format (follow exactly)
- **Section 1, "Summary":** at most 12 bullets, most useful for building the world first, each naming its claim numbers.
- **Section 2, "Claims":** one Markdown table, one row per claim, numbered:
  `| # | Claim | Confidence | Evidence | Phase and date | Builds | Source URL | Source date | Retrieved |`
  Claim: one sentence. Evidence: excavated, analysed, depicted, textual or inferred. Builds: what the world could build from it (terrain, sea, sky, building, interior, plant, animal, person, object, boat, colour, sound, event or timeline). One source per row; repeat a claim on another row if it has a second source.
- **Section 3, "Numbers for the builder":** one table: `| Quantity | Value | Unit | Range | Confidence | Claim # |`.
- **Section 4, "Assumptions checked":** for each assumption ID below, one line: confirmed, partly confirmed, contradicted (claim numbers), or could not verify.
- **Section 5, "Debates":** where specialists disagree, each position with its sources, a few lines each.
- **Section 6, "Game hooks":** design suggestions, kept apart from the evidence and clearly marked as suggestions.
- **Section 7, "Sources":** the full list with links.

Keep the narrative short. The tables are what will be used.
