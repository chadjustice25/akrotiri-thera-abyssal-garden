I am building a real-time 3D reconstruction of the island of Thera (Santorini) and its Bronze Age town at Akrotiri, as they stood in the last months before the Late Bronze Age ("Minoan") eruption. It runs in a web browser, and everything in it is generated by code (terrain, sea, buildings, plants, animals, boats, people and sound), with no photographs, textures or 3D model files. So I need concrete, sourced facts and numbers (shapes, sizes, materials, colours, quantities) that code can be built from, and an honest grade of how sure each one is. The project's time window is c. 1650 BCE, give or take 100–150 years (about 1800–1500 BCE). Later the same world will grow to other places at the same moment (Crete, Egypt, Byblos, Cyprus, the Levant, Anatolia, Mesopotamia), so every date must be stated in a way that can be compared across regions.

## Source rules (important)
1. Cite a source for every claim, with a link where one exists. Prefer the excavators' own publications (the Akrotiri excavation of the Archaeological Society at Athens: Marinatos, Doumas, Palyvou and the current team), peer-reviewed journals, conference volumes, museum catalogues and specialists' books. Tourist sites, popular articles and AI-written pages are not sources; use Wikipedia only to find the sources it cites.
2. Grade every claim's confidence:
   - **A:** found on Thera in a pre-eruption context (Late Cycladic I), or a scientific analysis of such material (pollen, charcoal, seeds, bone, residue, pigment, radiocarbon).
   - **B:** shown in Theran art of the time (it shows what Therans saw or imagined, not that it existed on the island); or found at a contemporary site elsewhere in the Aegean and reasonably carried over to Thera; or found on Thera but its interpretation is debated.
   - **C:** later evidence, analogy (later periods, other regions, ethnography), a modern reconstruction, or a hypothesis.
3. Shown is not present. For every plant, animal, object or landscape taken from a wall painting, say whether it is also physically attested on Thera.
4. Dates: give each piece of evidence its archaeological phase (e.g. Late Cycladic I, Late Minoan IA) and an absolute date range, say whether the range follows the "high" (radiocarbon) or "low" (Egyptian-linked) chronology, and how far it is from the eruption.
5. Numbers: whenever a source gives a measurement (size, height, depth, area, count, distance), give the number, its unit and its range as the source states them. If you convert units, say "converted".
6. Never guess. If you cannot find something, write "could not verify". Label your own reasoning INFERENCE. Mark single-source claims [verify]. Linear A is undeciphered: never invent a Minoan word or name, and say plainly when no ancient term is known.
7. Read only: no sign-ups, purchases or downloads.
8. Rights: when you cite a plan, map, photograph, 3D model or dataset, state its licence or terms of use if the source states them.

## LANE T1: Thera's land, sea and climate before the eruption

### Topics
A. **The island's shape before the eruption.** Compare the published reconstructions of pre-eruption Thera (for example Heiken and McCoy; Druitt and Francaviglia; and newer work from seafloor surveys and drilling, including IODP Expedition 398 if published) and the evidence each rests on. Was there an older caldera, how much of it was flooded by the sea, where were its openings, was there an island inside it, how high and wide was the ring of land, and what land has been lost since? Give elevations, depths and distances.
B. **What has stayed the same.** Which landforms existed then much as today (the non-volcanic basement of Profitis Ilias and Mesa Vouno, the older volcanic centres of the Akrotiri peninsula, Therasia, Aspronisi); how thick the eruption's pumice and ash lie over the old land surface across the island; how far the eruption deposits moved the coastline; and sea level c. 1650 BCE compared with today, including Santorini's own vertical land movement.
C. **Around Akrotiri.** Where the coastline and any harbour or sheltered bay lay relative to the town; the ravine or stream beside the site; slope, soils and fresh water (springs, wells, cisterns, rainwater); and the other known Bronze Age sites on Thera and Therasia, with their locations and what was found.
D. **Climate and weather** in the southern Aegean c. 1800–1500 BCE from palaeoclimate evidence (pollen cores, speleothems, marine cores): temperature, rainfall and its season, prevailing winds, and how they compare with today's.
E. **Data to start from.** Current elevation models and bathymetry covering Santorini (for example Copernicus DEM, EMODnet Bathymetry, published multibeam surveys of the caldera) with resolution and licence; published palaeogeographic maps of the pre-eruption island and their terms; and which present-day features must be removed (e.g. the Kameni islands) or added back to reach the pre-eruption shape.

### Assumptions to test
- **T1-1:** Before the eruption, Thera was a ring-shaped island around an older caldera that was largely flooded by the sea and open to it on at least one side.
- **T1-2:** An island stood inside that flooded caldera.
- **T1-3:** Profitis Ilias and the south of the island near Akrotiri had broadly today's shape, minus the eruption's ash and pumice cover.
- **T1-4:** Sea level c. 1650 BCE was within about 1–2 m of today's at Santorini.
- **T1-5:** Akrotiri was a coastal town with a harbour on the south coast.
- **T1-6:** The climate was Mediterranean much like today's, possibly a little wetter.
- **T1-7:** The Kameni islands did not exist yet; they formed after the eruption, from 197 BCE onward.
