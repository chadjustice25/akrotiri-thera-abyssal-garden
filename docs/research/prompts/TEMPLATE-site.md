I am building a real-time 3D reconstruction of the ancient world at one moment: c. 1650 BCE, give or take 100–150 years (about 1800–1500 BCE), shown at {SITE_NAME} ({MODERN_LOCATION}) as it stood in {SITE_NOW: the year or phase the world clock gives}. It runs in a web browser, and everything in it is generated by code (terrain, water, buildings, plants, animals, boats, people and sound), with no photographs, textures or 3D model files. So I need concrete, sourced facts and numbers (shapes, sizes, materials, colours, quantities) that code can be built from, and an honest grade of how sure each one is. The same world already contains Thera before its eruption, so every date must be stated in a way that can be compared across regions.

## Source rules (important)
1. Cite a source for every claim, with a link where one exists. Prefer the excavators' own publications ({EXCAVATION_TEAMS_AND_SERIES}), peer-reviewed journals, conference volumes, museum catalogues and specialists' books. Tourist sites, popular articles and AI-written pages are not sources; use Wikipedia only to find the sources it cites.
2. Grade every claim's confidence:
   - **A:** found at {SITE_NAME} in a context dated to {LOCAL_PHASES_IN_WINDOW}, a text of that time, or a scientific analysis of such material.
   - **B:** shown in the region's art of the time (it shows what people saw or imagined, not that it existed at the site); or found at a contemporary site elsewhere in {REGION} and reasonably carried over; or found at the site but its interpretation is debated.
   - **C:** later evidence, analogy (later periods, other regions, ethnography), a modern reconstruction, or a hypothesis.
3. Shown is not present. For every plant, animal, object or landscape taken from art, say whether it is also physically attested at {SITE_NAME}.
4. Dates: give each piece of evidence its local phase and an absolute date range, name the chronology it follows ({CHRONOLOGIES: e.g. the Egyptian chronology used; Middle Chronology}), and how far it is from {SITE_NOW}.
5. Numbers: whenever a source gives a measurement (size, height, depth, area, count, distance), give the number, its unit and its range as the source states them. If you convert units, say "converted".
6. Never guess. If you cannot find something, write "could not verify". Label your own reasoning INFERENCE. Mark single-source claims [verify]. {LANGUAGE_RULE: e.g. "Give Egyptian words in standard transliteration with their attestation; never invent a word or name, and say plainly when no ancient term is known."}
7. Read only: no sign-ups, purchases or downloads.
8. Rights: when you cite a plan, map, photograph, 3D model or dataset, state its licence or terms of use if the source states them.

## Report format (follow exactly)
- **Section 1, "Summary":** at most 12 bullets, most useful for building the world first, each naming its claim numbers.
- **Section 2, "Claims":** one Markdown table, one row per claim, numbered:
  `| # | Claim | Confidence | Evidence | Phase and date | Builds | Source URL | Source date | Retrieved |`
  Claim: one sentence. Evidence: excavated, analysed, depicted, textual or inferred. Builds: what the world could build from it (terrain, sea, sky, building, interior, plant, animal, person, object, boat, colour, sound, event or timeline). One source per row; repeat a claim on another row if it has a second source.
- **Section 3, "Numbers for the builder":** one table: `| Quantity | Value | Unit | Range | Confidence | Claim # |`.
- **Section 4, "Assumptions checked":** for each assumption ID below, one line: confirmed, partly confirmed, contradicted (claim numbers), or could not verify.
- **Section 5, "Debates":** where specialists disagree, each position with its sources, a few lines each.
- **Section 6, "Game hooks":** design suggestions, kept apart from the evidence and clearly marked as suggestions.
- **Section 7, "Sources":** the full list with links.

Keep the narrative short. The tables are what will be used.
