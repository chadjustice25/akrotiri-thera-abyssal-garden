Verification of lane T5 (docs/research/claude/T5_Claude.md), by a separate Claude verification agent on 2026-11-18 (D13).

Method: every one of the 27 source URLs in the report's Section 7 was downloaded again with curl on 2026-11-18 and read as text (PDFs through `pdftotext`). All 85 claim rows, every row behind a number in Section 3, and the INF distance arithmetic were checked against those texts. ## Verdict summary

- **Counts:** 85 claims checked (all of them). **77 Confirmed, 8 Partly, 0 Not supported, 0 Could not check.** All 27 URLs open (HTTP 200); 4 redirect to the same work at a new address (UCL, archaeology.wiki, AJA, Sail-World). No URL leads to a different work from the one cited.
- **Fabrication:** none found. Every number in the claims table occurs in its cited source. Every source is the work the report names. One **misattribution of method**: Summary bullet 3 and Debate 3 say the Heidelberg 9.7–23.6 m range comes "from 0.9 m paddle spacing". The Heidelberg page applies the 0.9 m spacing only to the earlier (late Early and Middle Bronze Age) boats, which come out at 7.6–15.6 m. For the frieze ships it gives 9.7–23.6 m from computer and wooden-model remodelling and states no method. Two citation details were wrong or open (Gale, Stos-Gale & Davis is Hesperia 53, 1984, not c. 1985; Merrillees is Aarhus 2009, pp. 247–251).
- **Ship dimensions are right and correctly attributed.** 9.7–23.6 m is Guttandin, Panagiotopoulos & Plath (Heidelberg 2010). 33.75 m is Spyridon Marinatos (1974a: 151), with 30 m in 1933, as reported by Shaw & Shaw 1999. 3.7–4.8 m beam at 24 m is Gillmer 1978: 125. 5.20–6.75 m at 33.75 m is Shaw & Shaw's own proportional scaling of Gillmer, not Gillmer's figure; the report says this correctly in #7. The ratio "1/5 to 1/6.5" in Summary 3 is correct arithmetic (24/4.8 = 5.0; 24/3.7 = 6.5).
- **Fleet counts reconcile.** Strasser's full JMA article (see "Works the report could not open") lists the frieze by Televantou's 1994 catalogue numbers. There are 7 large decorated ships with cabins (nos. 11–17): 6 are paddled (Wachsmann's "six processional ships"), 3 have masts up, and 1 (no. 15) has its sail set and no paddlers. The other 7 craft are 6 canoes (nos. 18–23) and 1 intermediate rowed craft with a canopy (no. 10). That makes 14 in all. So Strasser's "7 large" and Wachsmann's "6 processional" are the same fleet counted with or without the sailing ship. This is better than the report's "6–7" range.
- **Speeds and distances check out.** All 13 INF great-circle distances were recomputed and agree to within the stated ±5 or ±10 km (Akrotiri–Amnisos 114.6 km, i.e. 61.9 nmi). The crossing arithmetic is right: 62 nmi takes 10.3 h at 6 kn and 20.7 h at 3 kn. **But** Gheorghiade & Spencer think about 3 kn is the likelier Late Bronze Age speed. At that speed the crossing takes longer than a summer day's daylight, so "about a day" holds only at the faster speed or with night sailing. Also, 115 km sits at the bottom edge of the 100–150 km day's-sail limit, not comfortably inside it.
- **Metal provenance claims are faithful but narrow.** Lavrion lead at LC I Akrotiri is stated (in a Keos paper) exactly as reported. The LM IB Apliki ingots are as reported. Gale & Stos-Gale's own open 1992 summary (below) adds that Late Bronze Age Minoan and Mycenaean copper was mainly from Lavrion with **a little from Cyprus**. So "Cyprus unsupported for Akrotiri" stands, but "Cypriot copper only after the eruption" is too strong as a general statement. Nothing Akrotiri-specific on copper was found.
- **Can ships be built from it?** Yes, as grade-B hull types with grade-C dimensions, after the corrections below. Use the Strasser and Televantou breakdown for which ship carries what. Keep length as a parameter in a liberty: about 10–24 m (Heidelberg) up to 33.75 m (Marinatos). Beam is about 1/5 to 1/6.5 of the length (Gillmer, scaled). The number of paddlers per ship is still not sourced.

## Section 3 ("Numbers for the builder") checked row by row

- **Length of frieze ships (Heidelberg) 9.7–23.6 m:** the number is right. The method claimed in Summary 3 and Debate 3 is not on the page (see the verdict).

## Corrections

1. **Summary 3 and Debate 3:** remove "from 0.9 m paddle spacing" for the 9.7–23.6 m range. Heidelberg applies the 0.9 m spacing, with 1.65 m men, to the earlier boats (7.6–15.6 m) and gives the frieze range from model remodelling without stating a method.
2. **Summary 2 and the Section 3 fleet rows:** replace "7 (Strasser); 6 (Wachsmann)" with the reconciled breakdown: 7 large (6 paddled plus 1 under sail), 6 canoes and 1 rowed intermediate craft, 14 in all. All 7 large ships have cabins, and 3 have masts up.
3. **#23 and Summary 6:** "two at anchor" is M.C. and J.W. Shaw's reading; Marinatos read the two boats as leaving to meet the fleet. Also note that Heidelberg reads a quay-like waterline and boats beached stern-first in the arrival town. So "not moored at quays" is the majority reading, not settled.
4. **#35 and Section 3:** 131 ft is the depth of one raised amphora, not a maximum.
5. **#52:** no lead net weights at LC I Akrotiri. Privitera dates Aegean lead net weights from LH IIIA2. The trident is from Mochlos LM IB. Only the plant-fibre net (and a trammel-net identification, grade B) is from Akrotiri.
6. **#47:** narrow the claim to the illustrated bones (a photograph caption).
7. **#80:** there are two non-disc exceptions (a possible cross and a 1.4 cm cube). Michailidou's experiments support casting in moulds.
8. **T5-6 and Debate 7:** add Michailidou 1990's conclusion that the West House set is one set of balance weights, with no grounds for two systems. This weakens Petruso's c. 180 g system as a reason to doubt a single standard. T5-6 is better stated as "largely confirmed: the Minoan unit holds; some stone pieces may be foreign units".
9. **T5-7:** add that 3 kn, the speed the modellers think likelier, gives about 21 h, more than a summer day's daylight. Add that Heidelberg's wind study found good sailing links from Thera to north Crete on almost every day of the year (its "Thera triangle"; grade C), which supports the crossing.
10. **T5-5 and Summary 10:** keep "Lavrion lead" (A, [verify]). For copper, add Gale & Stos-Gale's open 1992 summary: Late Bronze Age Minoan and Mycenaean copper was mainly Lavrion with a little from Cyprus (grade B, carried over). Cyprus is unsupported **for Akrotiri** rather than shown to be absent before the eruption. Jones also has Cyprus entering the Cretan copper market by LC I–II.
11. **#18:** date the fuller hulls and paired steering oars to the Middle Minoan seals, as the Heidelberg page does.
12. **Citations:** Gale, Stos-Gale & Davis, Hesperia 53.4 (1984) 389–406. Merrillees, MoDIA (Aarhus University Press 2009) 247–251; the volume number was not confirmed, and it is probably the *Time's Up!* volume. Van de Moortel's paper is on pp. 329–336 and cites works of 2021, so it is c. 2021, in an unidentified volume (keep [verify]).

## Works the report could not open: what this verification found

- **Morgan 1988, *The Miniature Wall Paintings of Thera*:** no legitimate open copy found (Cambridge book). Reliable summaries were found in Strasser 2010 (full JMA article; the author's own Providence College URL now returns 404, and a copy of the published article was read). Strasser says the "emerging consensus" follows Morgan's reading of the frieze as a public display or nautical ceremony. He also reports Morgan's point that ships are zoned artificially into upper and lower lines. Shaw 1990 cites Morgan 1988: 161 for the walled arrival town and for identifying it as Akrotiri. **What it changes:** the ceremonial reading (T5-1) is the majority view, not one view among several. No paddler count is available from these summaries.
- **Stos-Gale & Gale 1990 ("The role of Thera in the Bronze Age trade in metals", TAW III vol. 1: 72–91):** no open copy (catalogue record only, zenon.dainst.org). The same authors' open summary, Gale & Stos-Gale 1992, "Lead isotope studies in the Aegean (the British Academy Project)", *Proceedings of the British Academy* 77: 63–108 (https://www.thebritishacademy.ac.uk/documents/3107/gale-stos-gale-isotope-studies-aegean.pdf), says: Late Bronze Age lead (and perhaps silver) moved from Lavrion through the Cyclades to Thera and Crete; Early Bronze Age copper came mainly from Kythnos; Late Bronze Age Minoan and Mycenaean copper came mainly from Lavrion, with a little from Cyprus. **What it changes:** T5-5 becomes "lead Lavrion (A, [verify]); copper probably mainly Lavrion by analogy (B); a little Cypriot copper possible (B); no Akrotiri copper analyses seen".
- **Wachsmann 2019, *Arts* 8(4): 165:** open at MDPI (the PDF downloaded from mdpi-res.com; the HTML page returns 403). Its Thera section repeats the 2011 points: six processional ships, decorated bowsprits, sails and sometimes masts lowered, awkward paddling, a rowed accompanying vessel, and a lashed stern device on the processional ships only. It dates the eruption to c. 1628 BC and gives no lengths or crew counts. It also warns that ancient images are not scale drawings and that human figures are often out of scale with the ships. **What it changes:** it confirms #1, #2, #10, #11, #15. It undercuts any length estimate taken from the painting's proportions, which bears on Marinatos's 33.75 m more than on the Heidelberg paddler-spacing method.
- **Wachsmann 1998 (book):** no legitimate open copy; it is cited through Van de Moortel (pp. 96–99, rigging), Strasser (pp. 94 and 108–113, paddling as an archaic cultic practice) and Wachsmann 2019. Nothing changes.

## Gaps

- **Crew:** no source gives the number of paddlers per side on any frieze ship. Morgan 1988 and Televantou 1994 are the places to look. Until then the crew count is a liberty.
- **Bow and stern debate omitted:** Heidelberg says the bow could be either end depending on the voyage's purpose (warlike end with ram and beast, or peaceful end with ornaments). This contradicts the Casson and Wachsmann directionality the report presents as settled (#16).
- **Ship appearance for the builder:** no hull colours, decoration motifs per ship (lions, dolphins, birds), sail colour or material, freeboard, or cabin (ikria) dimensions. The Room 4 ikria paintings (Doumas 1992, cited by Strasser) are not covered.
- **Anchors:** no stone anchors from Akrotiri or the Aegean of the period.
- **Balance finds:** Delta 16's two pairs of balance pans and its imports are missing (Michailidou 2006).
- **Metals:** no tin, gold or silver provenance for Akrotiri; Akrotiri copper is unanalysed in the sources seen.
- **Small boats and shellfish:** the canoes are counted (6) but not described; no Akrotiri shellfish species list (Mylona 2020 full text not opened).
- **Exports:** Thera's own exports beyond "port services" (Jones) are not covered.
