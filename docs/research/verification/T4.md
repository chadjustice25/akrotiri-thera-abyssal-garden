- **Sources: 32 of 32 open** (plus the two "find only" pages: the Dartmouth bibliography and the Ancient World Magazine review). Each is the work it is cited as. **No fabricated source, quotation or number was found.** Four small misattributions inside true rows: #45 (the bread-making and "more than one household" judgements are Devetzi's, in Appendix A, not Birtacha's), #76 (the seated man's paunch and white loincloth come from Gallou, not Fournier), #96 (the "toys" remark is not attributed in the review), #109 (Maxwell-Hyslop doubts that the *fresco dagger* was Cretan, not the inventory weapon). One detail is not in its cited source: "dolphinfish" in #38.
- **The three [verify] rows that rest on news reports (#51, #68, #85) say what their pages say, with one real error:** #68 is not a "triton-shell fragment". The newsroom page describes an inscribed fragment of a *likeness* of a triton (an object imitating the shell, material not stated). Keep all three at [verify] until the excavation's own report is read.
- **Kitchen (#39–#47), textiles (#7–#15) and Linear A (#58–#70): numbers match their sources exactly.** Corrections are about framing, not figures: the main hearth's interior was clean (soot belongs on Hearth 2 and the walls); the tuna's species is probable, not certain; the loom-weight thickness range and warp-density range are combined from several setups; the House of the Ladies pithos is not one of THE Zb 1–4.
- **People can be depicted from this lane, with the corrections below.** The report is careful: it grades every painted scene B or lower, flags single-scholar hypotheses as C, never invents a Minoan word, and labels modern nicknames. Its main over-reach risk is in the Game hooks and Summary, not the evidence: painted dress, nudity and hairstyles come from ceremonial scenes in a few rich buildings and should not be put on every townsperson; the "merchants' house" is Michailidou's hedged suggestion; and the age markers for old women (bulky bodies, red-streaked eyes) are painting conventions, not portraits.

### Depicting people: what the evidence allows

- **Dress, hair and skin are painted conventions from ceremonial scenes** in a few large buildings (Xeste 3, West House, Xeste 4). They are grade B for anyone, and they show festival or ritual costume, not what a farmer or potter wore at work. No opened source describes ordinary working dress. The world should not put open-bodice ceremonial dress, nudity or shaved heads on every figure; where figures appear, say they follow the paintings (standard §4.5).
- **Old age markers** (bulky bodies, red-streaked eyes, snoods) are painting conventions read by Davis, Younger and Gallou. Use them only with that label, never as a caricature of old people.
- **"Merchants" in Complex Delta** is Michailidou's hedged suggestion ("perhaps", "may have been"). The Game hook "The Delta merchants' house" should keep the hedge.
- **The rites** (initiation, menarche, healing, circumcision) are competing readings. The report rightly leaves them open; keep the viewer panel approach and never stage a specific rite as fact.
- **No bodies** were found, so faces, stature and health cannot come from Akrotiri. The report's "empty town" hook fits the evidence best.
