## The colour palette

Buildable now as **material tokens**, each carrying its claim number: lime-plaster white (#3; plaster, not a pigment), carbon black and probable manganese black (#4), haematite-rich red, clay-rich pink, orange, brown and yellow ochres (#5), vivid Egyptian blue (#12, #14), grey-blue riebeckite (#12), grey from clay + carbon (#6) or blue + black (#64), green from yellow ochre + Egyptian blue (#18; Xeste 3, rare), and murex purple (#20). Usage rules with evidence: white grounds and skies (#23), Egyptian blue for bright motifs and riebeckite for broad fields (#12, #24, #26), orange upper zones (#36), Egyptian blue over a riebeckite underlayer for the brightest blue (#14).

What it cannot give: any measured colour of a painted surface. Every RGB or linear value is a liberty until the gaps below are filled. ## Rights finding (topic F)

Research, not legal advice; a Greek lawyer should confirm before any sale.

- **Photographs and faithful images.** Under Art. 46 §4B of Law 4858/2021, producing or reproducing for public dissemination, for profit, an "image" of a State-owned monument always needs a prior licence and a fee to ODAP (§4, §5; procedure in MD 436630/2023). §4Δ defines "monuments" to include State-owned movable monuments in Ministry museums, which covers the Akrotiri paintings in Athens and Fira, and "image" as a faithful rendering of the monument's existing appearance on any medium, the internet and digital applications included. Wikimedia's own notices (#86, #87) read the law the same way: non-commercial online posting of non-professional photos is free; commercial use is not. Separately, modern photographs carry their photographers' or the excavation archive's rights (credit lines, #90; life + 70 under Law 2121/1993, #86). Greece's implementation of the DSM Directive's Art. 14 (no copyright in faithful reproductions of public-domain art) is expressly without prejudice to Law 4858/2021 (#85).
- **A motif re-created in code.** §4Δ(b) says a rendering that starts from the monument but goes beyond it creatively, by addition, subtraction, scientific interpretation, artistic creation or imagination, needs prior licensing only in the §4A cases (special access, bulky equipment, laser scanning or photogrammetry, special production conditions). A procedural re-creation built from published descriptions and measurements, restoring original colours and filling gaps, triggers none of §4A and sits on that side of the line. "Exact copies" (§4Γ) are physical replicas without completions and are not in play. A law-firm commentary reads it the same way (#84). No source settles how close a code re-creation may come before it counts as a faithful "image", and none deals with games; the report's INFERENCE in Section 4, T6-5, is a fair reading.
- **D6 is supported.** Committing the reference photographs to the public fork would publish images of State-owned monuments, made by others under their own copyright, with no licence on record, and the project may later be sold. Keeping them out of git, and re-creating motifs from checked descriptions rather than tracing photographs (standard §5), is the course the law and the institutions' terms point to.
