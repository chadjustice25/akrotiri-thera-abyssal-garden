That covers every row graded A or A/B except the Morgan ones, every row behind a number in "Numbers for the builder", the every-fifth sample and every surprising row. - **Confirmed: 47. Partly: 4** (#43, #45, #58, #63). **Not supported: 0. Could not check: 20** (the Morgan rows; 7 of them are partly backed by Strasser's summaries of Morgan: #22, #23, #29, #35, #61, #68, #69).
- **Sources: every URL opened** and each is the work it is cited as. The exceptions are the PMC pages (they opened by direct request but the fetcher refused the domain) and the Morgan upload (its page exists; its text was deliberately not read). The live Strasser link does return 404, as the report says, and the Internet Archive copy is the published JMA article. **No fabricated source, quotation or number was found.** One misattribution is in "Sources not opened": Friedrich et al. 1988 is *Naturwissenschaften* 75(11): 567–569, not *Bulletin of Volcanology* (Crossref, DOI 10.1007/BF00377720).
- **The central finding stands, with one correction of emphasis.** No source opened by the report or by this check identifies the fresco's left-hand island with the island in the caldera (Pre-Kameni). The geologist and archaeologist pair who make the caldera argument are W. L. Friedrich and A. Højen Sørensen, probably the pair behind Chad's recollection. They call the left-hand town's ground an "island-like area", a label that goes back to S. Marinatos (1974). They place it on the caldera wall of north Thera, in the dyke zone below Megalo Vouno at Mouzaki Bay. In a popular article (Videnskab.dk, 9 Aug 2010) they add that this is the zone where the eruption's crater lay "on a now-vanished island in the caldera". They suggest the island-like area was a piece of caldera wall that broke off and slid into the sea, or a projection of the wall. That is the closest any source comes to Chad's reading, and it is still not Pre-Kameni. No source puts buildings, temples or elite houses on the caldera island. Assumption T1b-1 is correctly judged "not supported as stated"; T1b-4 is correctly judged "could not verify".
- **Every number checked matches its source**, except two small rounding slips in the INFERENCE row. One modelling caveat is missing, and it matters for the terrain (correction 1 below).
- **Usable for the terrain work order: yes, with the corrections below.** The geology rows (Karátson 2018 and 2020, Hooft 2019, Druitt 2015, Karstens 2023, the Nomikou 2019 guide) are sound and match number for number. The fresco rows are usable as grade-B descriptions and grade-C readings, and none of them may set terrain geography. Pre-Kameni's height and footprint, the bay's depth, the channel's width and depth, and Akrotiri's harbour remain unknown. They must enter as liberties (§6 of the standard), not as evidence. The Morgan rows grade nothing until they are checked against the printed book.

## Corrections to apply before building

1. **The Pre-Kameni INFERENCE heights need a caveat, and two values are slightly off.** Scaling the Kameni shield by (V/4.3)^⅓ gives 2.5 km and about 340 m for 1.6 km³, 2.9 km and about 390 m for 2.4 km³, and 3.1 km and about 420 m for 3.0 km³. The report gives 2.6 km and 350 m, and 3.0 km and 410 m. These heights are above the **bay floor**. The Kameni shield's 470 m is measured from a floor about 300–390 m deep, and only about a quarter of it stands above the sea today. If the bay was only a few metres deep (#46), a shield-scaled Pre-Kameni would reach 340–420 m above sea level. That is higher than the 200–260 m rim of Karátson's DEMs (#51), and it disagrees with the "ideal low dome" those authors drew (Karátson 2018). The paraboloid row's 5 km base has no source. For the terrain: treat Pre-Kameni's height and footprint as a liberty, keep the summit below the rim unless a source says otherwise, and record the choice in §6. Soften the game hook ("rising a few hundred metres") accordingly.
2. **T1b-1: keep the verdict, and add the closest reading.** Add a row for Friedrich and Højen Sørensen's "island-like area" (2013, p. 153; Videnskab.dk 2010). In their reading it is semi-circular, lies on the north Thera caldera wall in the dyke zone at Mouzaki Bay, and is either a block of wall that slid into the sea or a projection of the wall. They note that this is the zone of the crater on the vanished caldera island, but they do not identify the two. The label "island-like area" comes from S. Marinatos 1974. This probably explains the belief the lane started from. Grade C.
3. **#45 and Summary bullet 2.** Strasser's remark that "no land is painted in the middle" concerns the eastern caldera face, not Pre-Kameni, and his reconstruction includes a Pre-Kameni. Say that the absence of a central island in the painting is the lane's own observation, and that it is a problem no caldera reading discusses.
4. **#26 against #31.** Morgan is reported both ways on whether the Departure Town's rocks look volcanic. Resolve this against Morgan 1988: 33–34 in print. Until then, Summary bullet 4 ("Morgan even reads the left-hand land as limestone") and T1b-2's verdict should not lean on #26.
5. **#63 and the Section 3 craft row:** 7 large ships, 6 canoes and 1 intermediate craft (Televantou No. 10) make 14.
6. **Debates 1, third bullet.** The Friedrich group does not pair Balos and Raos as the two towns. In the 2010 popular article both lie near the **right** harbour, and the left harbour is in north Thera. Reword to: Mouzaki Bay to Balos (2013), with Balos and Raos named as matching sites (2010, 2014 abstracts).
7. **#43:** grade A → B.
8. **#58:** the count of 17 double-harbour sites is Doumas's own admission, as reported by Strasser.
9. **Morgan rows (20):** do not adopt any until each is checked against the printed book (Cambridge Classical Studies, 1988) or Doumas 1992 / Televantou 1994. This includes the frieze length of 3.90 m, the town areas and the 567 m summit. The archive.org URL must not appear in project data. Rows 29, 35 and 68 can cite Strasser in its place for the parts he covers.
