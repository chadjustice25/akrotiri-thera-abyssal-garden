# **Lane T3d: Plants in Aegean Art (1800–1500 BCE) and What They Can Prove for 1615 BCE Thera**

For a procedural 3D reconstruction of Akrotiri set in late May 1615 BCE (High radiocarbon chronology), Aegean wall paintings and ceramics provide reliable blueprints for interior mural shaders, vessel iconography, and artistic colour palettes—but cannot be used alone to populate the outdoor Theran landscape. Archaeobotanical seeds and wood charcoal show that pre-eruption Thera supported barley and pulse agriculture, olive and almond orchards, open oak woodland, pine, juniper, and stream-side alder and tamarisk—none of which dominate the town's murals—while the flowers painted most prominently on Akrotiri's walls (lilies, crocuses, sea daffodils/papyri, and palms) lack confirmed Bronze Age macro-remains on the island and mix botanical observation with artistic convention, hybrid morphology, and multi-season compression. Because Linear A remains undeciphered, no contemporary Minoan or Theran names for any of these plants are known.

## **Section 1: Summary**

* **Never place a plant outdoors on Thera from art alone:** Of the plants prominent in Akrotiri murals and pottery, only barley, pulses/vetches, olive, fig, pine (*Pinus* tp. *brutia*/*halepensis*), and grape (via resinated-wine residue) have physical pre-eruption backing on Thera; lilies, saffron crocuses, sea daffodils, papyrus, date palms, reeds, myrtle, ivy, wild roses, and osiers lack confirmed Bronze Age macro-remains on the island (Claims 4–20, 45–59).  
* **Use wood charcoal and stored seeds to drive outdoor terrain generation:** Pre-eruption LC I charcoal reveals a far greener, more wooded island than rocky floral murals imply—including open oak woodland, olive, pine, juniper, cultivated almond, pear, pistachio/terebinth, sweet chestnut, fig, and riparian alder and tamarisk pointing to a real freshwater stream, alongside fields of hulled barley and pure crops of *Lathyrus clymenum* and *L. ochrus* (Claims 45–50).  
* **Respect late-May 1615 BCE phenology when separating murals from live flora:** INFERENCE — Saffron crocuses bloom only for a few days in autumn and sea daffodils peak in late August, so neither would flower outdoors in late May 1615 BCE; only late-spring lilies could be in bloom, whereas frescoes deliberately compress multiple seasons into permanent backdrops (Claims 11, 16, 41, 42).  
* **Anchor your cross-regional timeline explicitly to High vs. Low chronologies:** Your 1615 BCE target sits inside the High (radiocarbon) eruption window (1627–1600 BCE; 1613 ± 13 BCE; Doumas c. 1615 BCE), roughly 0–50 years after Akrotiri's post-earthquake LC I rebuild and contemporaneous with MM IIIB–LM IA Crete, Ayia Irini Period VI, Phylakopi LC I, and Trianda LM IA, whereas Low Egyptian-linked chronology shifts the eruption to c. 1530–1520 BCE (Claims 1, 2, 32–40).  
* **Build Akrotiri's interior floral shaders from analyzed mineral and organic pigments (Grade A):** Use local volcanic hematite/goethite ochres (including Athinios orange at 68% natrojarosite, 23% hematite, 9% quartz), synthetic Egyptian blue layered over or mixed with natural Mg-riebeckite (blue-grey), Egyptian blue \+ yellow ochre (with hornblende also detected) for green lily foliage, and *Bolinus brandaris* murex purple on a calcite/aragonite lake restricted at Akrotiri to Xeste 3 crocus petals and garments (Claims 22–26).  
* **Code mural plant dimensions by architectural register, not botanical scale:** Megalographic murals span 2.05–2.50 m wall zones (Xeste 3 Goddess H. 2.44 m; Delta 2 Spring Fresco full-wall across a 2.10 × 2.50 m room; Amnisos Lily Fresco H. 1.87 m), whereas narrative friezes shrink to 40–44 cm (West House Flotilla N/S walls) and \<20–21 cm (West House East Wall river landscape), with palms carrying 20–40 fronds out of scale to their surroundings (Claims 4, 11, 14, 18, 19, 36).  
* **Reproduce artistic conventions rather than literal botany inside murals:** Paint crocus leaves yellow (not green) with dried blood-red stigmas; keep Delta 2 red lily stems straight (usually 3 stems/clump with simultaneous buds and blooms); use stencil-drawn white-blue and red double-spiral ivy in Xeste 3 (5 m long); and expect hybrid or contested taxa such as Knossian papyrus-reed-lily hybrids and the House of the Ladies papyrus vs. sea daffodil debate (Claims 4, 5, 15, 16, 34, 41).  
* **Treat date palms and papyrus differently:** Papyrus (*Cyperus papyrus*) is an Egyptian artistic import absent from Bronze Age and modern Cycladic wild flora, whereas the endemic Aegean date palm (*Phoenix theophrasti*) grew on Pleistocene Santorini (\~37,000 BP) and still grows wild on Crete and Amorgos—meaning palms were regionally known even though Bronze Age Theran remains are absent (Claims 6, 18, 55, 56).  
* **Match Akrotiri's painted ceramics to physical storage and portable planters:** Model LC I nippled ewers with barley ears, stirrup jars with vetch, bichrome *kymbai* and stuccoed tripod offering tables with 3-petal/3-stigma crocuses or marine seaweed, the "Lilies Jug" and floral pithoi, and decorated terracotta flowerpots with base-only drainage holes meant to sit visibly on floors and windowsills (Claims 17, 27–31).  
* **Do not model manicured palace gardens on Thera:** No archaeobotanical or pollen evidence currently confirms physical gardens anywhere in Bronze Age Crete or Thera; even the stepped architectural base under Amnisos's *in cavo* white lilies \[T15\] is a debated Cretan-only hypothesis (Confidence C for Thera), whereas freestanding painted flowerpots are physically excavated at Akrotiri (Claims 30, 36, 43).  
* **Avoid placing specific real-world Theran landmarks into painted landscapes:** No Akrotiri mural can be securely shown to depict a particular spot on Thera—the West House East Wall river is a composite "Aegean Nile scene" and rocky grounds follow pan-Aegean polychrome conventions—even though West House riparian charcoal (*Alnus*, *Tamarix*) indicates a stream likely existed somewhere on the island (Claims 43, 44, 50).  
* **Do not invent Minoan or Theran plant names:** Linear A remains undeciphered; keep plant labels in English or botanical Latin and clearly flag any Greek term as post-eruption Linear B or Classical (Claim 3).

## **Section 4: Assumptions checked**

* **T3d-1:** **Confirmed** — Akrotiri's lilies, crocuses, and sea daffodils/papyri combine natural traits (3-stigma autumn crocuses; simultaneous lily buds and open blooms) with artistic convention (yellow crocus leaves, blood-red dried stigmas, rigidly upright lily stems, debated *L. candidum* \+ *L. chalcedonicum* composite) alongside documented multi-species hybrids on contemporary Crete (Claims 4, 5, 11, 16, 34, 41).  
* **T3d-2:** **Partly confirmed** — Confirmed that *Cyperus papyrus* derives from Egyptian art and neither papyrus nor date palm is physically attested on Bronze Age Thera, but contradicted as to date palm's exclusively foreign origin because endemic *Phoenix theophrasti* grows wild on Crete and Amorgos (Cyclades) today and *Phoenix* fossils occur on Pleistocene Santorini (\~37,000 BP) (Claims 6, 16, 18, 55, 56).  
* **T3d-3:** **Confirmed** — Akrotiri LC I pottery depicts ears of barley (Sector A nippled ewer) and vetch (House of the Ladies stirrup jar), and both hulled barley and pulses (*Lathyrus clymenum*, *L. ochrus*, *Vicia ervilia*) are physically excavated as stored seeds and flour at Akrotiri (Claims 27, 28, 45, 46).  
* **T3d-4:** **Confirmed** — MM III–LM IA / LC I wall paintings overwhelmingly place flowers on polychrome undulating rocks or marsh edges while omitting cultivated grain fields, vineyards, and orchards (barring isolated trees, trees in enclosures, and the proposed walled garden at Amnisos), even though archaeobotany attests extensive cereal, legume, olive, and almond cultivation (Claims 4, 8, 11, 18, 19, 33, 36, 43, 45–50).  
* **T3d-5:** **Confirmed** — Specialists treat Akrotiri's floral and riverine paintings as idealized conventions or composite "Aegean Nile" / generic Aegean scenes rather than identifiable Theran topography, though riparian *Alnus* and *Tamarix* charcoal indicates a real stream likely existed somewhere on the island (Claims 43, 44, 50).  
* **T3d-6:** **Partly confirmed** — Maria Shaw (1993, abstract) and the Heraklion Archaeological Museum interpret the stepped architectural structure under Amnisos's *in cavo* white lilies \[T15\] (plus possible wooden-planter fragments) as a probable formal walled garden, but the mural is heavily burnt and no archaeobotanical or pollen evidence confirms any physical Minoan garden (Confidence C for Thera) (Claims 36, 43).

## **Section 5: Debates**

**House of the Ladies Room 1: Papyrus vs. Sea Daffodil.** Warren (1976, cited via Morgan 1988 and Nugent 2014), Morgan (1988), and Doumas (1992) identify the tall stylized plants spanning three walls of Room 1 as Egyptian-derived papyrus (*Cyperus papyrus*), citing rhizome, sheath, and bract details alongside Egyptian-Aegean parallels4. Porter (2000), Diapoulis (1990), and Baumann (1993) (all cited via Nugent 2014\) counter-identify them as native Greek sea daffodils (*Pancratium maritimum*), arguing that the clumping white-tipped blooms match coastal late-summer sea lilies whose intense evening fragrance suited ritual settings10. Neither species is physically attested in Bronze Age Theran remains.  
**Delta 2 Spring Fresco Red Lilies: Botanical Hybrid vs. Colour Licence.** Sarpaki (2000, cited via Nugent 2014\) argues that Akrotiri's red lilies combine traits of the white Madonna lily (*Lilium candidum*) and the red Chalcedonian lily (*Lilium chalcedonicum*), whereas Hollinshead (1989, cited via Forsyth) favoured *L. chalcedonicum*10. Baumann (1993, cited via Nugent 2014\) contends the plant is solely *L. candidum* coloured red by artistic licence for visual contrast against a white plaster background10. Modern *Flora of Greece* checklists record *L. chalcedonicum* as native only to mainland mountains and *L. candidum* mostly as a cultivated escape on Aegean islands, leaving Bronze Age wild presence on Thera unconfirmed61.  
**Xeste 3 Crocuses: Wild *Crocus cartwrightianus* vs. Cultivated *Crocus sativus*.** Sarpaki (2000, cited via Nugent 2014 and Day 2011\) and Day (2011) argue that Xeste 3's rocky mountainous terrain depicts wild harvesting of native *Crocus cartwrightianus* rather than cultivation10. Conversely, Younger (2014) reads the clumps as "planted for humans to pluck," and Kazemi-Shahandashti et al. (2022) report cytogenetic and genotyping-by-sequencing data supporting an autotriploid origin of sterile *C. sativus* in Greece from two cytotypes of *C. cartwrightianus* (\>99% shared alleles; \~98% in Attica accessions), interpreting Xeste 3's clumped long-stigma blooms as early cultivation—though art alone cannot separate the two59. On Xeste 3 Ground Floor Room 3's East Wall, Vlachopoulos (2008) reads the red streaks on the altar's horns of consecration as blood drops beside an olive(?) tree, whereas Rehak (2001) interprets the red streaks as saffron stigmas13.  
**Date Palms in Aegean Art: Regional Endemic vs. Foreign Exotic.** Traditional scholarship traced Minoan and Theran palms to Egyptian or Near Eastern *Phoenix dactylifera*. Morgan (1988) observes that *P. dactylifera* and Greuter's endemic Cretan date palm (*Phoenix theophrasti*) are visually indistinguishable in the same climatic zone, making iconographic separation insoluble17. Because *P. theophrasti* still grows wild on Crete and on Amorgos in the Cyclades and Pleistocene *Phoenix* fossils (\~37,000 BP) occur on Santorini itself, Aegean painters did not require foreign travel to observe palms—yet the lack of LC I Theran palm charcoal prevents placing them confidently outdoors in 1615 BCE64.  
**West House Miniature Frieze Setting: Thera, Crete, Libya, or Composite.** Spyridon Marinatos proposed a North African ("Libya") setting and read the curved palms as wind-bent; Morgan (1988) refutes both (palms curve simultaneously left and right for compositional rhythm), locating the main narrative in the Aegean and defining the East Wall river as a composite "Aegean Nile scene"17. Strasser (2001) interprets the South Wall flotilla as a local nautical ceremony rather than a long-distance voyage, while Bottema-Mac Gillavry (2004) shows that alder (*Alnus*) and tamarisk (*Tamarix*) charcoal from the West House supports the real existence of a Theran freshwater stream—without demonstrating that the mural maps a specific Theran location18.  
**Minoan Gardens: Formal Architecture vs. Absent Physical Proof.** Shaw (1993, abstract) and the Heraklion Archaeological Museum read Amnisos's stepped lily fresco \[T15\] as a likely formal walled garden, and Shaw proposed a trimmed bedrock "rock garden" in the east wing at Phaistos44. Hitchcock (2000, cited via UCD garden study) rejects the Phaistos rock-garden reading, and the UCD archaeological synthesis notes that no palaeobotanical or pollen evidence currently verifies any physical Minoan garden—suggesting portable, decorated ceramic flowerpots (physically excavated at Akrotiri) and wooden tubs substituted for soil beds in paved courts32.  
**Absolute Chronology of the Thera Eruption: High vs. Low.** Radiocarbon dating of a tephra-buried Santorini olive branch (1627–1600 BCE; 1613 ± 13 BCE) and IntCal20 Bayesian modeling with informative priors (Manning 2024: very late 17th to early 16th c. BCE) anchor the High chronology adopted for your 1615 BCE world1. Egyptian-linked historical synchronisms (New Kingdom start c. 1540 BCE; Cypriot White Slip I bowl at Akrotiri) support the Low chronology (c. 1530–1520 / 1525 BCE; Bietak & Höflmayer 2007; Wiener 2009; Driessen 2019), while mid-16th c. BCE radiocarbon ranges (1570–1544 / c. 1560 BCE; Pearson et al. 2018; Poursat 2022\) occupy an intermediate position1.

## **Section 6: Game hooks**

*All items below are design suggestions kept strictly separate from archaeological evidence.*

* **Dual-Pipeline Flora Engine ("Wall Shader" vs. "Terrain Biome"):** Split your procedural generator into two strict registries. Populate exterior 1615 BCE hillsides, terraces, and stream beds solely from Grade A archaeobotanical species (pruned olive and almond groves, barley and *Lathyrus clymenum* fields, grazed maquis with juniper and pistachio, open oak woodland, *Pinus brutia*/*halepensis* timber, and an alder/tamarisk stream edge), while reserving lilies, crocuses, sea daffodils, papyrus, and blue palms for interior plaster shaders and ceramic decals.

Outdoors, barley fields approach harvest and late-spring lilies could sit visibly in Akrotiri's base-draining painted terracotta flowerpots on windowsills (echoing West House Room 4), whereas autumn-blooming *Crocus cartwrightianus* and late-summer *Pancratium maritimum* appear exclusively inside Xeste 3 and House of the Ladies frescoes—accompanied by a note explaining how Aegean painters compressed separate seasons into permanent ritual rooms.
