# **Wall Paintings, Pigments, Pottery, Visual Conventions, and Reproduction Rights at Late Cycladic I Akrotiri (Thera)**

To build a procedural, code-generated 3D reconstruction of Late Bronze Age Thera (Santorini) and Akrotiri in the final months before the Minoan eruption (Late Cycladic I / Late Minoan IA, c. 1650–1600 BCE on the High radiocarbon chronology or c. 1600–1500 BCE on the Low Egyptian-linked chronology), your engine can rely on concrete architectural, dimensional, archaeometric, and geometric parameters established by the Akrotiri Excavations1. Nearly all preserved figurative wall paintings decorated upper-storey formal rooms (with the exception of the ground-floor Lustral Basin and vestibule/corridors in Xeste 3, the ground-floor Room Delta 2 *Spring Fresco*, and the ascending entrance staircase of Xeste 4), executed on multi-layered calcitic lime plaster (roughly 1 cm thick, range 1–2.5 cm, over a 1–2 cm mud-straw backing) in a hybrid *buon fresco* and *a secco* technique using taut-string horizontal zone lines, incised sketches, and seven recurring mathematical stencils (1 Archimedes' spiral, 4 hyperbolae, and 2 ellipses)1.  
Your procedural shaders need a six-material base palette—polished calcite lime white, carbon or pyrolusite manganese black, local goethite yellow ochre, local Athinios hematite/natrojarosite red-orange ochre, Cycladic magnesio-riebeckite grey-blue, and imported cuprorivaite Egyptian blue—supplemented in elite Xeste 3 compositions by mixed blue-yellow greens and *Bolinus brandaris* murex purple (*purpurissum*)3. Because your browser application generates all geometry and shaders algorithmically without embedding photographs, textures, or 3D scans, it uses zero copyrighted assets (as 3,600-year-old motifs are in the public domain under Greek Law 2121/1993 Article 29), though you should account for the statutory ambiguity in Greek Law 4858/2021 Article 46 and Joint Ministerial Decision 126463/2011 if distributing a commercial game inside Greece12.

## **Section 1, "Summary"**

* **Upper-Floor Placement and Ground-Floor Exceptions (Claims \#6–\#39):** Place most painted rooms on the first or second upper storeys (West House Rooms 4 and 5; Building Beta Rooms B1 and B6; House of the Ladies Room 1; Xeste 3 first-floor Rooms 2, 3a, 3b, 4 and second-floor Rooms 3 and 9), reserving ground-floor murals for the small interior Room Delta 2 (*Spring Fresco*, 2.10 × 2.50 m), the Xeste 3 Lustral Basin/corridors (Rooms 3a, 3b, vestibule), and the ascending entrance staircase of Xeste 41.  
* **Tripartite Wall Zoning via String Lines (Claims \#7, \#8, \#20, \#51):** Divide standard painted walls horizontally using taut-string impressions into three zones: a lower dado/socle (painted as veined marble orthostats or a monochrome band), a main pictorial zone (1.00–2.00 m high centered on a 1.50 m eye-level horizon), and an upper border frieze of bands, running spirals, or ivy leaves2.  
* **Plaster and Paint Layer Geometry (Claims \#4, \#43–\#46):** Model interior wall finishes as a 1–2 cm mud-and-straw backing coat (*arriccio*), a \~1 cm (range 1–2.5 cm) calcitic lime plaster body, a thin floated fine lime *intonaco* (0.5–0.7 cm or a few mm), and a 10–100 µm pigment layer executed in *buon fresco* for backgrounds/main figures and *a secco* for fine details and murex purple5.  
* **Mathematical Stencils for Procedural Contours (Claims \#52, \#53):** Generate figure contours, floral leaves, and running spirals piecewise using the exact mathematical parameters measured on Akrotiri murals (mean error 0.26–0.36 mm): Archimedes' spirals (r \= κθ with κ \= 1.393 cm, 0.6263 cm, 0.2256 cm, and 0.1693 cm), four hyperbolae (a\_H, b\_H), two ellipses (a\_E, b\_E), and 24-, 32-, and 48-gon radial divisions4.  
* **Six-Pigment Base Palette and Two-Layer Blues (Claims \#45–\#48):** Build procedural room shaders from lime white (CaCO₃), carbon/pyrolusite black (C / MnO / Mn₃O₄), local goethite yellow ochre (4–12.1% α-FeO(OH)), local Athinios hematite red ochre (1–52% α-Fe₂O₃) and natrojarosite orange (68% natrojarosite \+ 23% hematite), Cycladic Mg-riebeckite grey-blue, and synthetic Egyptian blue (CaCuSi₄O₁₀), applying fine Egyptian blue as a thin topcoat over a coarse riebeckite underlayer to conserve imported pigment3.  
* **Elite Murex Purple and Mixed Greens in Xeste 3 (Claims \#49, \#50):** Restrict true molluscan purple (*purpurissum*, 65.5–71.5% 6,6'-dibromoindigo from *Bolinus brandaris* on crushed shell aragonite/calcite) and mixed blue-yellow greens to high-status ritual compositions such as Xeste 3 crocus petals, necklaces, and woven garments9.  
* **Strict Skin and Scalp Colour Code (Claims \#9, \#18, \#25, \#58):** Shade adult and adolescent male skin in red-brown hematite ochre, toddler boys (aged 4–5 years) in brownish-yellow ochre, and all females in unpainted white lime plaster with red lips/cheeks and red eye-streaks; render the shaved scalps of youths and children in blue (riebeckite/Egyptian blue) with 2 to 4 uncut black hair locks2.  
* **Theran Polychrome Rockwork Without White Highlights (Claims \#31, \#32, \#58):** Draw volcanic rocks as undulating, cellular bands of alternating strong red, yellow, and blue-grey with black internal veining lines and **zero white highlights** (unlike Cretan paintings), stylizing Thera's real hydrothermally oxidized volcanic and metamorphic cliffs2.  
* **Shown vs. Physically Present Flora and Fauna (Claims \#11, \#12, \#40–\#42):** Populate the island's physical 3D ecosystem with bioarchaeologically attested species—olives (*Olea europaea*), sedges (*Cyperus* sp.), bog-rush (*Schoenus nigricans*), cereal/legume crops, sheep, goats, cattle, red deer antlers, coastal Sparidae fish (*Pagrus pagrus*, *Dentex dentex*), picarels, and swallows—while keeping monkeys (*Chlorocebus* and *Papio*), East African antelopes (*Oryx beisa*), lions, and griffins strictly inside painted interiors as exotic/mythical motifs15.  
* **Final-Phase Synchronism of Beta 6 and West House (Claims \#1–\#5, \#14, \#34):** Render both the Building Beta Room B6 *Blue Monkeys* and the West House Room 5 *Miniature Frieze* as intact, contemporaneous murals of the town's final LC I architectural phase prior to the eruption5.  
* **Last-Phase Pottery and Stone Vessels (Claims \#55–\#57):** Populate rooms with predominantly local Theran pottery (Bichrome, Polychrome, White-on-Dark, and Dark-on-Light nippled ewers with swallows, boat-shaped *kymbai*, reed- and lily-painted *pithoi*, strainers, and bathtubs) and \~15% imports (LM IA Cretan ripple/spiral wares and rhyta, LH I mainland cups, a Cypriot White Slip I bowl), plus local dacite/andesite tripod mortars (and a 1.30 m lava pithos) alongside imported veined marble chalices and ostrich-egg rhyta1.  
* **Procedural Code Rights vs. Greek Law 4858/2021 (Claims \#59–\#61):** Because Bronze Age artworks are in the copyright public domain and your engine generates motifs in code without using photographs or 3D scans, no copyright licence is needed; however, under Greek Law 4858/2021 Article 46 and JMD 126463/2011, commercial digital applications reproducing State antiquities face permit/fee claims in Greece (20% of retail price), whereas non-commercial educational websites qualify for ministerial fee waivers and software-generated reconstructions occupy an acknowledged statutory grey area12.

## **Section 4, "Assumptions checked"**

* **T6-1 ("Most Akrotiri wall paintings were painted at least partly in true fresco on wet lime plaster"):** **Confirmed** (Claims \#43, \#44, \#46, \#50) — Cross-sectional microscopy, SEM-EDS, and XRD show that primary backgrounds and main pigment layers (including coarse Egyptian blue) were applied *buon fresco* onto wet floated calcitic lime plaster, while secondary details and murex purple (*purpurissum*) were added *a secco*5.  
* **T6-2 ("The palette is mostly red and yellow ochres, Egyptian blue and other blues, lime white and black, with few or no greens"):** **Confirmed** (Claims \#45–\#50) — The base palette consists of calcitic lime white, carbon and manganese-oxide blacks, local goethite yellow ochre, local hematite/natrojarosite red-orange ochres, Cycladic magnesio-riebeckite blue, and Egyptian blue; pure green earth (celadonite/malachite) is absent on Akrotiri walls, with greens restricted to blue-plus-yellow mixtures in Xeste 3 and purple made from *Bolinus brandaris* murex dye3.  
* **T6-3 ("Painted rooms were mostly on upper floors"):** **Confirmed** (Claims \#6–\#39) — Nearly all residential and ritual wall paintings adorned first- or second-storey rooms (West House Rooms 4–5, Building Beta Rooms B1 and B6, House of the Ladies Room 1, Xeste 3 first and second floors), with ground-floor murals limited to Room Delta 2 (*Spring Fresco*), the Xeste 3 Lustral Basin/vestibule/corridors (Rooms 3a–3b), and the Xeste 4 staircase1.  
* **T6-4 ("Rocks and landscape are painted as bands of strong, varied colours, not in the island's real colours"):** **Partly confirmed** (Claims \#31, \#48, \#58) — While rocks are stylized into undulating Aegean bands of alternating red, yellow, and blue-grey with black veining lines and no white highlights, archaeometric and geological studies show these strong hues reflect Thera's actual hydrothermally oxidized red hematite/scoria, yellow goethite/natrojarosite veins (as at Athinios), and dark grey-blue volcanic/metamorphic cliffs2.  
* **T6-5 ("Reproducing these paintings in a commercial product needs permission from the Greek state, and a re-creation of a motif in code may too"):** **Partly confirmed** (Claims \#59–\#61) — Reproducing photographs or direct depictions of State-owned antiquities in a commercial product requires a Ministry of Culture permit and ODAP fee under Greek Law 4858/2021 Article 46 par. 4 and JMD 126463/2011 (20% of digital app retail price), whereas re-creating 3,600-year-old public-domain motifs purely in procedural code without photographs infringes no copyright, is generally outside Greek administrative jurisdiction internationally, and falls into an acknowledged statutory grey area inside Greece because JMD 126463/2011 ties fee schedules to "photographs"12.  
* **T6-6 ("The Beta 6 monkey paintings and the West House miniature frieze both belong to the town's final phase before the eruption"):** **Confirmed** (Claims \#1–\#5, \#14, \#34) — Stratigraphic restudy of Building Beta confirms that the Room Beta 6 *Blue Monkeys* fresco was painted over an earlier plaster substratum during the second and final LC I architectural phase of the building, making it contemporaneous with the B1 *Boxing Boys*/*Antelopes* and the West House *Miniature Frieze* in the Volcanic Destruction Level5.

## **Section 5, "Debates"**

> 1. **Absolute Date of the LC I Wall Paintings and Minoan Eruption (High vs. Low Chronology):**  
   * *High (Radiocarbon/Dendrochronology) Position:* Manning (2014) and Höflmayer (2014) place the LC I Volcanic Destruction Level in the late 17th or very early 16th century BCE (c. 1650–1600 BCE or c. 1628–1560 BCE), arguing that the single pre-eruption Cypriot White Slip I bowl from Thera predates the residual WS I sherds found in Thutmose III levels (Stratum C) at Tell el-Dab'a2.  
   * *Low (Traditional / Egyptian-Linked) Position:* Wiener (2001, 2003), Bietak (1998, 2003), and Eriksson (2001) date the eruption to c. 1550–1500 BCE, arguing that Cypriot White Slip I ware and LM IA interconnections only appear in Egypt and the Levant during the early 18th Dynasty32.  
> 2. **Extent of *Buon Fresco* vs. *A Secco* Execution:**  
   * *Predominantly Buon Fresco Position:* Brysbaert (2006, 2008\) and Asimenos (1978) emphasize that floating pure calcitic lime plaster brought calcium hydroxide to the surface, locking in even coarse-grained Egyptian blue while the plaster remained wet (aided by thick mud-straw backing layers that retained moisture for days)5.  
   * *Hybrid Fresco-Secco Position:* Perdikatsis et al. (2000, 2021), Chryssikopoulou et al. (2000), and Sotiropoulou (2005) demonstrate that while backgrounds and primary zones were painted *buon fresco*, post-depositional osmotic pressure can mimic pigment penetration and secondary layers, details, lime-water washes, and murex purple (*purpurissum* tempera) were executed *a secco*9.  
> 3. **Botanical Identity of the House of the Ladies Plant (Papyrus vs. Sea Daffodil):**  
   * *Papyrus (Cyperus papyrus) Position:* Marinatos, Doumas (1992), Warren, and Sarpaki (2000) identify the tall triangular umbels in Room 1 of the House of the Ladies as stylized Egyptian *waz* papyrus plants (noting that *Cyperus* sp. sedges are archaeobotanically attested on Thera)29.  
   * *Sea Daffodil (Pancratium maritimum) Position:* Porter (2000) and Nugent (2014) argue that the blue flower heads with 7 protruding yellow anthers represent pressed blossoms of the native Aegean coastal sea daffodil (*Pancratium maritimum*, which has 6 anthers in nature), hybridized with papyrus stems63.  
> 4. **Primatological Identity and Live Presence of Monkeys on Thera:**  
   * *Live Imported Pets / Two Genera Position:* Doumas (1992), Trantalidou (2000), and Urbani & Youlatos (2012, 2020, 2022\) distinguish naturalistic vervet monkeys (*Chlorocebus* spp.) in the Beta 6 landscape from anthropomorphized baboons (*Papio* spp.) in Xeste 3 ritual scenes, arguing that the lifelike locomotion and frontal expressions in Beta 6 indicate Theran artists observed live vervets brought as exotic pets from Egypt30.  
   * *Iconographic Transmission / Absence of Bones Position:* Other scholars note that zero primate bones have ever been excavated at Akrotiri or Crete, suggesting artists may have worked from Egyptian/Near Eastern visual models or travellers' accounts rather than resident island populations30.  
> 5. **Geography and Meaning of the West House Miniature Frieze:**  
   * *Local Theran / Cycladic Nautical Festival Position:* Doumas (1992), Morgan (1988, 2007), Strasser (2001, 2010), and Friedrich & Højen Sørensen (2010, 2013\) interpret the South wall Flotilla as a peaceful seasonal or religious maritime procession between two local Theran harbours (with Town V representing Akrotiri or Balos inside the pre-eruption caldera/coastline)2.  
   * *Overseas Expedition / Epic Narrative Position:* S. Marinatos (1974) proposed an overseas expedition to Libya, while Warren (1979) and Morris (1989) view the frieze as a generalized Aegean epic anthology combining coastal raids (North wall), subtropical riverine exoticism (East wall), and triumphant homeward navigation (South wall)2.
