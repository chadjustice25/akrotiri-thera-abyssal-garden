# **Botanical Morphology, Phenology, and Chronology for Pre-Eruption Thera**

Procedural reconstruction of pre-eruption Thera in late May (\~1615 BCE, high radiocarbon chronology) must replace Santorini's modern treeless terraces with a relatively moist, cool Bronze Age mosaic of open deciduous oak woodland (probably *Quercus macrolepis*), cattle-grazed Mediterranean maquis, riparian *Alnus* and *Tamarix* along an inferred freshwater stream, and both cultivated *Olea europaea* trees and compact, twisted olive shrubs1. Inside the \~20 ha urban core of Akrotiri, archaeobotanical deposits attest cleaned wheat, barley (including cracked barley and flour), bitter vetch (*Vicia ervilia*), *Lathyrus clymenum*, probable white lupin (*Lupinus cf. albus*), broad bean (excerpted \[verify\]), flax (*Linum usitatissimum*), coriander (*Coriandrum sativum*), garlic (*Allium sativum*), and structural *Ficus carica* earthquake-repair wood, whereas uncleaned barley and legumes in chaff ("straw") belong at peripheral farmsteads with sheep-and-goat pens like Balos6. For botanical motifs drawn from Akrotiri wall paintings—none verified as Theran macro-remains in the available evidence—coders must correct fresco colour conventions (where yellow ochre replaces light green and Egyptian Blue replaces deep green \[verify\]) and withhold blooms from autumn-flowering crocuses and late-August-flowering sea daffodils in a late-May build12.  
Major quantitative and seasonal gaps in the available primary literature restrict direct procedural generation and are flagged plainly rather than guessed. Adult height, spread, bark, leaf dimensions, fruit colours, and exact Cycladic leafing/flowering/fruiting calendars could not be verified for most attested Theran trees and crops (olive, fig, grapevine, oak, pine, tamarisk, pear/hawthorn, cereals, most pulses, flax, coriander), nor could late-May harvest states, landrace straw heights, or annual grass desiccation10. Where verified, post-Bronze Age Mediterranean introductions—prickly pear (*Opuntia*), *Agave*, *Citrus*, *Zea mays*, and cultivated *Pistacia vera*—must be excluded, alongside unverified or doubted taxa such as carob (*Ceratonia siliqua*)17.

## **Section 1: Summary**

* **Anchor the island ecology in grazed maquis, open oak woodland, and riparian corridors rather than modern treeless vineyards.** Excavated charcoal indicates open oak woodland (probably *macrolepis*), cattle-grazed maquis, and riparian *Alnus*, *Tamarix* sp., and *Castanea* alongside cultivated olive dating back to the 3rd millennium BCE (Claims 3, 4, 5, 6, 7\)1.  
* **Generate two distinct *Olea europaea* growth forms.** Buried tephra remains include small caldera-area shrubs on Therasia with twisted stems 8 mm to c. 2.5 cm thick (2–3 mm bark; bands \< 400–500 µm), alongside upright olive trees at 172 m asl next to a Bronze Age wall on Santorini (Claims 8, 9\)3.  
* **Zone cereal and pulse states strictly between urban Akrotiri and rural outliers.** Store only clean grains, split pulses, and flour inside the \~20 ha town, while placing uncleaned barley and legumes in chaff beside animal pens at peripheral estates modeled on Balos (Claims 12, 14, 16\)10.  
* **Use *Lathyrus clymenum* and *Satureja thymbra* where concrete morphological parameters exist, and flag unverified crops.** *Lathyrus clymenum* has phyllodic lower leaves, winged upper petioles, and straight 52–80 × 8–9 mm pods, while *Satureja thymbra* forms a 20–40 cm × 0.4 m rocky-hill subshrub blooming in May–June; dimensions and May states for other attested crops and scrub could not be verified (Claims 15, 17, 19\)23.  
* **Do not treat painted flora as present on Thera or in May bloom without physical corroboration.** Lilies, crocus, sea daffodil/papyrus, sedge, reeds, myrtle, ivy, palm, and grape appear in Akrotiri art without confirmed Theran macro-remains here, and neither autumn-flowering *Crocus* nor late-August-flowering sea daffodil blooms in late May (Claims 21, 22, 23, 24\)13.  
* **Reverse Theran fresco colour substitutions before texturing foliage.** Aegean mineral palettes (haematite, ochre, mineral black, Egyptian Blue over lime plaster) led Theran artists to substitute yellow for light green (myrtle) and deep blue for deep green (ivy, papyrus, lily, reeds, palm) \[verify\] (Claim 20\)12.  
* **Model *Phragmites australis* stands along the inferred stream using two-year stem layering, noting the Cycladic gap.** Modern *Phragmites australis* reaches 1–6 m (2–4 m typical) with persistent tan previous-year stems, drooping blue-green leaves (20–60 cm × 8–50 mm), and 15–50 cm panicles, though whether it was the exact wild Cycladic reed painted at Akrotiri could not be verified (Claims 6, 25, 26\)5.  
* **Include \~50-year-old earthquake-repair timber and imported woods in Akrotiri buildings.** Structural *Ficus carica* wood reflects emergency repairs after the late MC/early LC I earthquake (\~50 yr pre-eruption; c. 1665 BCE high INFERENCE), complemented by cedar, yew, and beech imported from Lebanon, Cyprus, and Anatolia (Claims 2, 11, 18\)1.  
* **Exclude confirmed post-Bronze Age introductions.** Prickly pear (*Opuntia*), agave (*Agave*), and *Zea mays* reached the Mediterranean after 1493 / 16th century CE, *Citrus* spp. arrived from antiquity through the 19th century CE, and *Pistacia vera* cultivation reached Greece in the 19th century CE (Claims 29, 30, 31\)17.  
* **Omit carob (*Ceratonia siliqua*) and flag unverified trees.** Carob is unattested on Thera and doubted for ancient Europe by Grove & Rackham, while cypress (attested in Crete architecture), pomegranate, almond, and date palm remain unverified on Bronze Age Thera (Claims 33, 34\)21.  
* **Document chronological ambiguity and undeciphered Linear A in metadata.** Radiocarbon plateaus and olive-ring false bands pit the \~1615 BCE high chronology against mid/low \~1540 or 1525–1490 BCE dates, and no Bronze Age Theran name for Akrotiri or its plants is known (Claims 1, 10\)28.

## **Section 5: Debates**

**Chronology of the Minoan Eruption (High \~1627–1600 / \~1615 BCE vs. Mid/Low \~1540 / 1525–1490 BCE):** Doumas28 (\~1615 BCE), Manning et al.31, and Friedrich et al. (2006, 2014\)3 uphold a late-17th-century BCE eruption date anchored by 14C wiggle-matching and unconstrained ordered sequences on a Santorini olive branch (1627–1600 BCE; 1656–1609 BCE unconstrained). Conversely, Cherubini et al. (2014)32 argue that mild \~10 °C Santorini winters induce false olive growth bands (44% average deviation across 10 experts, yielding 40–104 years for 72 rings) and that dead wood cannot be ruled out, keeping 1525–1490 BCE viable, while Pearson et al. (2018) / PNAS (2020)31 and Pearson et al. (2023)4 document a \~1600–1530 BCE calibration plateau and slightly younger outer radiocarbon measurements on a Therasia olive shrub favouring a mid-16th-century BCE (\~1540 BCE) eruption.  
**Red Lilies in Room Delta 2 (*Lilium* Hybridization vs. Artistic Convention):** Sarpaki (cited in nugentvol21.pdf)13 proposes that the red lilies on the Spring Fresco fuse traits of white Madonna lily (*Lilium candidum*) and *Lilium chalcedonicum*. Baumann (cited in nugentvol21.pdf)13 counters that the species is purely *Lilium candidum*, explaining both the red petal hue and stiff upright stems—rather than naturally drooping flowering stems—as Aegean artistic convention (paralleling Doumas 1992: 18 on limited fresco palettes, cited via Wikipedia12 \[verify\]).  
**House of the Ladies Motif (Sea Daffodil vs. Papyrus) and Xeste 3 Seasonality:** Nugent's compilation (nugentvol21.pdf, citing Porter)13 identifies the triadic coastal blossoms in the House of the Ladies as late-August-flowering sea daffodils, whereas other scholarship interprets them as stylized Egyptian *waz* papyrus or an Aegean hybrid (Morgan 1988\)13. Separately, the *Hesperia* 80(3) study14 and nugentvol21.pdf13 demonstrate that Xeste 3 crocus frescoes portray autumn-flowering *Crocus cartwrightianus*/*C. sativus*, overturning earlier claims of spring or summer symbolism.  
***Lathyrus ochrus*** **at Akrotiri and Carob (*Ceratonia siliqua*) in Ancient Greece:** A peer-reviewed synthesis (PMC12956113)37 reports that both *Lathyrus clymenum* and *Lathyrus ochrus* were stored in pure lots at Akrotiri, contradicting the *Annual of the British School at Athens* synthesis on Crete36, which states that *L. ochrus* has never been recorded from prehistoric Greece outside Crete. For carob (*Ceratonia siliqua*), archaeological remains from Jericho (8000–6000 BCE), Mount Carmel, and 12th-dynasty Egypt support an eastern Mediterranean presence possibly spreading westward in the 2nd millennium BCE, whereas Schweinfurth placed its wild origin in Yemen/Horn of Africa and Grove & Rackham (2001: 69\) doubt the tree was known in European antiquity21.
