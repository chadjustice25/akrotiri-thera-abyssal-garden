## How grades are applied in this lane (read first)
This lane applies the T1 landscape rules (T1, "How grades are applied") and adds three for the painting:
- **The painting as an object** is grade **A**: it was found on Thera in a Late Cycladic I context (the West House, Room 5), so its measurements and the pigments the painters used are A.
- **What the painting shows** is grade **B** at most, under "shown is not present" (`docs/HISTORICAL_STANDARD.md` §4.2). A published description of a painted feature is B.
- **An identification of a painted place with a real place** (for example "the town on the right is Akrotiri") is grade **C**: it is a hypothesis about the painting, and the specialists disagree.
- **Names.** "Departure Town" and "Arrival Town" are modern labels for the towns on the left and the right of the frieze. Strasser and Karátson et al. use them even though they argue that no voyage is shown. "Pre-Kameni", "Strongyli/Strongyle", "Ship Procession" and "Flotilla Fresco" are modern labels too. Raos, Balos, Mouzaki Bay, Oia, Manolas and Alafouzos are modern place names. No Bronze Age name for any of these places is known.
- **Dates.** The fresco was painted in Late Cycladic I, before the eruption. The geological papers give the eruption as c. 3.6 ka. This lane does not choose a chronology; that is for W1/W2.

**The reference photograph.** The image `akrotiri-frescoes-sources/ship-procession-fresco-akrotiri-749.jpg` was viewed during this run and was not copied anywhere. Section 3b describes what it shows, and ties each feature to a published description.

## Section 1: Summary
- **The caldera reading is real, but it is a minority view and its own supporters disagree about where the places are.** Two groups make the case. The first is the art historian T. F. Strasser (2010), whose reading was adopted by the volcanologists Karátson et al. (2020), Strasser among them. The second is the geologist W. L. Friedrich with the archaeologist A. Højen Sørensen (2010, 2013, 2014). The three published mappings put the two towns in different places: Raos and southern Therasia (Strasser), Oia and Manolas on Therasia (Karátson 2020, "tentatively"), and Mouzaki Bay and Balos (Højen Sørensen et al. 2013). (#1–#21)
- **No source puts the island in the caldera into the fresco.** Every caldera reading places the left-hand town on the caldera rim of the main island, not on Pre-Kameni. Strasser notes that the frieze shows no land in the middle. The "ring of water" around the left town is read by almost everyone as a river or stream with two branches: a delta (Morgan), two watercourses (Doumas, Warren), a stream splitting around a town on a cliff (Strasser), or winter rainwater running down the caldera wall (Højen Sørensen et al.). Assumption T1b-1 is not supported as stated. (#2, #5, #10, #17, #25, #45)
- **The majority reading is that the right-hand town is Akrotiri itself and the left-hand town is a nearby, smaller settlement.** This view goes back to Page (1976) and Doumas (1983) and is set out at length by Morgan (1988). Shaw identified the double harbour with Akrotiri, and Doumas (2007) argued for harbours on either side of the site. Strasser and the Friedrich group reject the identification. (#7, #16, #22, #38, #58)
- **The rock colours are partly Theran and partly convention.** Morgan links yellow, pink and red, and blue to ash and sulphur, pumice, and dark andesite, and cites Rackham on a black, red and yellow lava flow about 1 km west of Akrotiri. But the painters had only three pigments (red ochre, yellow ochre, blue) plus blue-black, and banded multicoloured rocks are a Minoan–Mycenaean convention. Horizontal bands around the left-hand town are unusual. That is Strasser's strongest geological argument. (#4, #10, #26–#30)
- **Pre-Kameni: the volume and the rock are known, but not the height or the footprint.** It was black glassy andesite (a few per cent flow-banded rhyolite), 1.6–3.0 km³, about the size of today's Kameni islands (3.2 km³). It probably stood in the northern caldera. One study puts the first-phase vent on it, 1–2 km west of modern Fira. No height or footprint is published. Scaled from today's Kameni shield (4.3 km³, 3.5 km across at the base, rising 470 m above the floor), it would rise roughly 250–400 m above the bay floor (INFERENCE, grade C). (#39–#44, Section 3)
- **There is no evidence of buildings or any human use on Pre-Kameni.** The island is known only from lithic clasts in the eruption deposits. Assumption T1b-4 could not be verified, and the fresco is not evidence for it. (#45)
- **The bay was shallow, perhaps only a few metres deep.** The stromatolites put the bay at "no more than a few meters" of water (Hooft et al. 2019, citing Anadón et al. 2013 and Friedrich et al. 1988). Druitt's field guide, however, says the volume budget needs a "large and deep" depression before the eruption. The two views have not been reconciled. (#46–#48)
- **The northern opening was narrow, if it existed at all. Its width and depth are not published.** Cosmogenic dating suggests the northern breach is a long-lived feature. The southwestern breaches formed in the eruption. Today's NW channel is 1 km wide and about 300 m deep, but it was cut after the eruption, so it is only an upper bound. (#49, #52, #53; T1#10)
- **The ring's rim was modelled at 200 m or 260 m above sea level** in Karátson et al.'s two DEMs, which favour 260 m. Thera's highest point today, Profitis Ilias, is 567 m (Morgan 1988), and it is built of non-volcanic marble. (#51, #55, #56)
- **Akrotiri's harbour is still unknown.** Morgan places it in a bay west of the town (citing others). Doumas (2007) argued for harbours on both sides of the site. Strasser argues that the caldera, about 1 km to the north, was the better harbour. Building one sheltered south-coast bay and keeping the caldera in view is defensible; neither is evidence. (#57, #58, #7; T1#36–#37)
- **The frieze itself: about 3.9–4 m long and 43–44 cm high.** It shows 14 craft, among them seven large ships, and at least 15 dolphins. The left-hand town occupies 38 × 18 cm and the right-hand town 50 × 30 cm. Section 3b gives a feature-by-feature description for the builder. (#60–#71)

### 3b. What the fresco shows: a description for the builder (grade B throughout)
Read left to right, as the reference photograph shows it. Each feature names the published description it rests on. Features seen in the photograph but not described in any source opened are marked "seen only".

1. **Upper left: hills and animals.** Rocky, scrub-covered hills fall towards the left-hand town (#69). A forest of flat-crowned pines stands on them (#68), and a yellow lion stalks fallow deer along the hilltops (#68). Palm-like trees on the hills: seen only. Morgan describes palms on the east-wall river frieze, not here.
2. **Left: the Departure Town on its "island".** The town is a tight group of buildings, at most 38 × 18 cm of painting (#62). It sits on red, pink and ochre ground (#15, #30) inside a blue band with T-shaped projections. Most authors read the band as a river or stream that splits and joins the sea (#5, #25). Tall grasses line the water and the shore (#67). The ground is banded horizontally, in red, ochre and pink layers (#4).
3. **The left town's buildings.** Mostly blue rendered facades with slit windows in single or double pairs. Upper storeys show coursed blocks (probably mud brick) over rendered lower storeys. There are corner-stones in alternating tones and a two-storey tower (#64). The tilted "cornerstones" on one house are read by Friedrich et al. as dykes (#15). Small outlying buildings, and men in hairy capes at the town edge: Morgan's figure list, not described in detail here.
4. **Below the left town.** A long pink-ochre spit or peninsula runs to the right along the lower part of the frieze (#2, #9: "spits"/"capes"). A boat is painted in front of it and overlapping it (#6).
5. **The middle: the sea.** It is the bare light plaster ground with no painted water colour: seen only, consistent with #30 (white ground). Seven large ships in two rows (three above, four below), six smaller craft (#63), and dolphins mostly in the upper half (#6). One large ship has its sail set; the others are paddled. Festoons hang from the ships (#10). Leaping fish below the centre: seen only.
6. **Right of centre: the bright rocks.** A peninsula of brilliantly coloured rock (blue, red, yellow, in vertical bands and swirls) shelters the right-hand harbour (#69; colours #27). This is the feature most reminiscent of Thera's volcanic rock (#27, #28), but also the standard Aegean rock convention (#29).
7. **The harbour.** Two kinds of mooring: large ships moored on one side of the promontory and small boats drawn up on a beach on the other (#38). Friedrich et al. describe a two-basin harbour (#16).
8. **Upper right: hills and structures.** Three hills (#70), with lookout posts on the hilltop (#65), a three-room building on one hill (#70), the terraced "apiary" structure with a road up to a tripartite hilltop building (#37), and rocks hanging down from the top edge (#69). Animals and figures on the upper-right hills: seen only.
9. **Far right: the Arrival Town.** Fills 50 × 30 cm (#62). A massive ashlar wall and gateway (#65, #66), with horns of consecration at two points (#66), and storeys stacked in a steep bird's-eye view (#65). Facades are blue, ochre and red, some decorative (#65). There are windows with lattices, and people on flat roofs, at windows and on balconies (#66). A line of men stands along the town wall, and women appear in the town: Morgan's figure list, not described in detail here.
10. **What the frieze does not show.** No island in the middle of the water (#45). No cliff wall facing the viewer across the water (#2). No smoke or other sign of volcanic activity: seen only.

## Section 4: Assumptions checked
- **T1b-1** (the island on the left of the frieze is the island in the caldera, and the scene shows the caldera): **contradicted in its first part; the second part is a debated hypothesis (grade C).** No source opened identifies the left-hand landmass with Pre-Kameni. All three caldera readings put the left-hand town on the rim of the main island: near Raos (#3), at Oia (#11) or at Mouzaki Bay (#14). The "ring of water" is read as a river or stream (#5, #10, #17, #25). Strasser notes that the frieze shows no land in the middle (#2, #45). That the scene shows the caldera at all is argued by Strasser and Karátson et al. (#1, #9) and by Friedrich's group (#14, #20, #21). It is opposed or not accepted by the majority reading (#22–#24) and by Chapin and Shaw (#32).
- **T1b-2** (the coloured rocks match Thera's volcanic rocks): **partly confirmed.** Thera does show black, red and yellow rock (#27, #28), and the horizontal bands around the left-hand town are unusual in Aegean painting (#4). But the painters had a three-pigment palette (#30), and banded multicoloured rock is an Aegean convention used far from volcanoes (#29). Morgan even reads the left-hand land as limestone (#26). No study was found that matches painted colours to specific Theran outcrops.
- **T1b-3** (the town on the right is Akrotiri): **debated; the majority view, not established.** For: Page 1976, Doumas 1983 and Morgan 1988 (#22), Shaw (#16), and Doumas 2007 (#58). Against: Strasser (southern Therasia, #3, #7), Karátson et al. 2020 (Manolas, #11), and Friedrich's group (Balos, #14, #16). Grade C either way.
- **T1b-4** (the island in the caldera carried buildings): **could not verify.** Pre-Kameni is known only from lithic clasts and volume estimates (#40–#43). No source opened reports a find, building or use on it, and no reading of the fresco places a town on it (#45).

## Section 5: Debates
1. **Is the frieze a picture of the caldera?**
   - *Yes, the caldera from the east rim:* Strasser (2010). The view is from the interior east rim looking west, with the Departure Town at Raos and the Arrival Town on southern Therasia (#1–#8). It rests on palaeotopography plus three iconographic oddities: the horizontal rocks, boats and dolphins above the land, and a single sail.
   - *Yes, the caldera entrance:* Karátson et al. (2020), with Strasser. The landmasses are the capes of the single NW entrance, seen from the SE, tentatively Oia and Manolas (#9–#13). The authors list what the reading still has to explain (#12).
   - *Yes, inner-caldera harbours:* Friedrich and Højen Sørensen (2010) and Højen Sørensen et al. (2013). They place the towns at Mouzaki Bay and Balos (2013), or name Balos and Raos (2010, 2014 abstracts) (#14–#21). This is the "geologist and archaeologist" pairing.
   - *No: an Aegean coastal view, with Akrotiri as the Arrival Town:* Morgan (1988), following Page and Doumas (#22–#27, #35). The coast type is generic Aegean, the towns are short-distance neighbours, and the rock colours reflect awareness of the island, not a map.
   - *No single viewpoint:* Chapin (1995), as reported by Strasser (#32).
   - *A voyage elsewhere:* Marinatos (Libya, #34); Negbi, Marketou and Warren (Libya, Rhodes, Crete, #33); Vance Watrous (an epic, #33).
   - *Status:* the caldera reading is published in a peer-reviewed volcanology journal (#9) but not adopted by the art-historical mainstream represented here. Its own supporters place the towns in three different pairs of places. That is the main weakness: the geology makes a caldera view possible, but the painting does not pin down where.
2. **The Arrival Town: Akrotiri or not?** See T1b-3. Shaw's double harbour (#38) and Doumas's (2007) harbours on both sides of the site (#58) support Akrotiri. Strasser counters that the caldera was the better harbour, and that Doumas's reconstruction ignores the lower sea level (#7). The harbour itself has never been found (T1#37).
3. **The water around the left town.** A delta (Morgan, #25), two watercourses (Doumas 1983 and Warren 1979, as reported in the 2013 paper), a stream around a cliff town (Strasser, #5), or winter rainwater down the caldera wall (Højen Sørensen et al., #17). Nobody reads it as the sea around an island in the caldera.
4. **How deep was the bay?** "No more than a few metres" (Hooft et al. 2019, from the stromatolites, #46). Druitt (#48) requires a large and deep depression to balance the volumes, while also accepting shallow water. Karátson et al. (2020) say the relation between the collapsed volume and the water depth is hard to assess. Karstens et al. (2023) needed only about 14 km³ of tuff to fill the northern basin (#50). One way to fit both, unverified, is a large depression mostly filled by Pre-Kameni and sediment, leaving shallow water. That is INFERENCE.
5. **Where did Pre-Kameni stand?** In the northern caldera, at the later crater (#19), or 1–2 km west of modern Fira (#39), or in the centre of the bay (T1#15). These are compatible if the bay was small and northern.
6. **The rock of Pre-Kameni.** Andesite in Karátson et al. and Hooft et al. (#40, #43); "dacite" in Druitt's 2015 guide (#41). The chemistry (high-Ba, low-Zr) is the same group; the name differs.
7. **Where was the opening?** West (Friedrich et al. 1988), or north/northwest, narrow, if any (Karátson et al. 2020; Karstens et al. 2023, after Athanassas et al. 2016) (#49, #52). The NW view of the fresco reading depends on the northern option (#9, #11).

## Section 6: Game hooks (suggestions only, not evidence)
- *Suggestion:* Build Pre-Kameni as a dark, glassy-black andesite island about 2.5–3 km across, rising a few hundred metres from a shallow bay in the north of the caldera. Record its height and footprint as liberties in the standard's §6 table, citing the Kameni-shield analogy (#44) and the INFERENCE rows above. Give it no buildings or paths, and if a viewer asks, say that nothing is known of any use.
- *Suggestion:* Do not model the frieze's left-hand town on Pre-Kameni. If the world shows the frieze's places, use a toggle between readings: "Akrotiri and a neighbour" (Morgan), "the caldera entrance" (Strasser and Karátson), and "Mouzaki Bay to Balos" (Friedrich). Label each as a hypothesis (grade C).
- *Suggestion:* Use the frieze for look, not geography. Its building vocabulary is well tied to excavated Akrotiri (#64–#66): blue-rendered lower storeys with slit windows, coursed upper storeys, alternating quoins, flat roofs with people, and lattice windows. Its harbour arrangement (#38) is usable for a south-coast bay. Mark both B in-world.
- *Suggestion:* Paint the inner caldera cliffs in horizontal bands of red, ochre, pale pink and dark grey, and give one south-coast headland the black, red and yellow lava look Rackham described (#28). The source of each is geology plus Morgan, not the fresco alone.
- *Suggestion:* Keep the northern channel narrow and shallow (a liberty) and the bay pale and shallow over light sediment. A distant grey-black island in the middle would be visible from the rim, as the fresco never shows it.
- *Suggestion:* A "ceremony on the water" event (festooned, paddled ships with one sail, crowds on roofs) can run as a seasonal festival in spring or autumn (#21, #23, #33), labelled as one interpretation.
