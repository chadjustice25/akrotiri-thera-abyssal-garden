# T8: the history of the modern excavation at Akrotiri

Brief: `docs/research/prompts/T8-excavation-history.md`, everything below its line. Its "Delivery" section was not followed, by instruction. Every row cites a page or file opened during this run (2026-11-20, running into the early hours of 21 November). Works that could not be opened are listed at the end of Section 7 and grade nothing. Quotations are under 15 words; Greek is paraphrased in English.

How it was researched: the excavators' own annual reports were read in Greek, in the scans published by the Archaeological Society at Athens (Η εν Αθήναις Αρχαιολογική Εταιρεία) and by the University of Thessaly repository. These are *Πρακτικά της εν Αθήναις Αρχαιολογικής Εταιρείας* (ΠΑΕ, "Proceedings of the Archaeological Society at Athens") for 1967–1971 and 1974, and *Το Έργον της Αρχαιολογικής Εταιρείας* ("The Work of the Archaeological Society") for every year from 1967 to 2022. The scans were text-searched through their OCR layer. Readings that matter (the 1970 start date, the name "Xeste E") were checked against page images. Later accounts by the director, one peer-reviewed study, the Ephorate's and the museums' pages, and press reports fill the gaps.

## How this lane reads the grades and dates (read first)
- **Grades follow the brief (modern-excavation reading).**
  - **A** is a statement in the excavators' report of that season (ΠΑΕ or Έργον), or a plan or photograph dated in it. Έργον entries are compiled by the Society's secretary general from the excavator's report; they count as the excavation's official publication.
  - **B** is a later account by the director or a team member: Doumas's 2016 book, his 2013, 2014, 2017 and 2019 pieces, and the foreword of the 40-years volume. A peer-reviewed study that cites the reports (Tzachili and Karnava 2016) is also B, and so is any point on which the excavators' own accounts disagree.
  - **C** is an Ephorate or museum web page, a press report, or this lane's own INFERENCE.
  - Rows about plans, terms of use or datasets carry **licence** or **data** instead of a grade.
- **Dates** are calendar years of the modern excavation. The month or day is given where a source states it. Where a find's age is mentioned, only its published phase is given (Early, Middle, Late Cycladic); no absolute date and no chronology is applied in this lane.
- **"Begun" is not "finished".** "Year begun" is the first season in which a trench, shaft or clearing touched the building, street or square. Later seasons are listed separately. No building is said to be finished unless a source says so.
- **Names are modern labels.** No ancient name is known for any building, street or square, or for the town.
  - Marinatos first used the landowners' names (Bronos, Arvanitis, Nomikos), then Greek letters from 1969 (Sector Α, Β, Γ; Δ in 1970).
  - Marinatos named Telchines Street (1969), the West House and the "Room of the Women" (1971), the Triangle (1972), and Xeste 2 and Xeste 4 (1972), with Xeste 3 named by 1973. The rows give the source for each.
  - Doumas's team named the House of the Benches (1993), Cenotaph Square (named by 1994) and Curetes Street (in use by 1995).
  - Linear A is undeciphered. The reports print a reading of signs on one jug; it is not repeated here.
- **"INFERENCE"** marks this lane's reasoning, always graded C.
- **OCR caveat.** The scanned volumes' text layer has errors. Room numbers and dates quoted here were read in context. Where a reading was doubtful, the page image was checked: 1 August 1970 and "Xeste E" (Ergon 1971, fig. 234).

## Section 1: Summary

- **Order in which the named buildings, streets and squares were begun** (first trench, shaft or clearing; details in Section 3):
  - **1967:** Complex Delta's north front, by the very first trench, Bronos 1/1a, on 25 May (#10, #17, #44, #146). The Pithoi Storeroom (Sector Alpha), from 27 May (#14, #16). Complex Beta (trench Bronos 2) (#18). Xeste 2 (trench Arvanitis 2) (#20, #65).
  - **1967–1969:** Building Gamma, which lay in the Bronos 2 sector and was named Γ in 1969 (#34).
  - **1969:** Telchines Street, named and cleared (#34, #35). The court at its north end, probably today's Millhouse Square (#40, #147). A Middle Cycladic pyre in what became Cenotaph Square (#41, #94).
  - **1970:** Complex Delta excavated as a whole, named Sector Δ (#44–#47). The north-east corner of the "West building", later the West House, first seen (#46).
  - **1971:** the House of the Ladies (#51). The West House (#53). Triangle Square (#52). Delta's South house (#54, #57). The southernmost "Xeste E", probably Xeste 3 (#58, #59). The "East Court", probably Double Horns Square (#60, #61).
  - **1972:** Xeste 4 (#66). **1973:** Xeste 3 named and half dug (#67). **1974:** Xeste 3's west wing (#69).
  - **Before 1975, year unknown:** Xeste 5's west wall partly exposed (#84).
  - **1977:** the "northern extension of Telchines Street", which is probably today's Dactyls Street (#76–#78). **1984:** most of Double Horns Square cleared (#84). **1985 and 1992:** the cenotaph cairn and figurine hoard (#85, #91).
  - **1993:** the House of the Benches named (#92). **1995:** Curetes Street (#95).
- **Where the first trenches were.** All lay along the bed of a north–south torrent. The first was "Bronos 1", begun on Thursday 25 May 1967 at the locality Favata, in Kalliope Bronou's field on the west side of the ravine; it is now inside Complex Delta's area (#10, #44). Two days later "Arvanitis 1" was opened c. 80 m to the north-east, east of the ravine (#14). Bronos 1a, Bronos 2 (under 50 m south), five trenches in the Nomikos field (east bank) and Arvanitis 2 (c. 30 m south of Arvanitis 1) followed in the same season (#17–#20). The French had dug at the same locality, "Favatas", in 1870 (#2, #3).
- **What cannot be dated to the year:**
  - House of the Anchor, "South Building", "Porter's Lodge" (Building Eta) and the cobbled side street: no source dates their first exposure (#139, #140, #142).
  - Xeste 5: only "by Marinatos", that is 1967–1974 (#84). Building Gamma: 1967–1969 (#18, #34).
  - The year the names Dactyls Street, Double Horns Square and Millhouse Square were given, and the year the tuff horns were found (#78, #144, #145).
- **How the dig grew.** It did not spread from one centre.
  - 1968–1969: two separate roofed areas, Sector Α in the north and Sectors Β–Γ in the south, with an unexcavated "bridge" between them (#25, #34).
  - 1970: the bridge was dug as Sector Δ to join them (#44, #45). 1971: the shelters joined Δ to Α (#51).
  - Then the dig spread west (West House, Triangle Square, 1971), to the far south (Xeste E, 1971) and to the south-east (Xeste 2 and Xeste 4, 1972) (#52, #53, #58, #65, #66).
  - Method: trial trenches (1967) (#12); then open digging under the torrent bed with tunnels on either side (1968–1969) (#24); then roofed open areas on pillar footings up to 7 m deep (#37).
- **Directors.** Spyridon Marinatos directed 1967–1974; he died at the excavation in 1974, aged 73 (#70). The Society's board then gave the excavation to Christos Doumas, whose first season, 1975, was spent only on arranging the site and the finds (#73). Doumas is still named as director in the latest Society report found that covers Akrotiri, for 2020 (#130). Ergon 2021 and 2022 have no Akrotiri section, and Ergon 2023–2025 would not open. **Who directs today could not be verified** (#131, #132).
- **Pace.**
  - By 1974 Marinatos had cleared c. 1 ha and begun eight buildings but finished none (#72). The site today is c. 3 acres, about 1.2 ha (converted) (#123).
  - Doumas's seasons went deeper rather than wider: West House 1976–1990; House of the Ladies 1971–1992; Xeste 3 1973–1994; Delta 1970–1996; Xeste 4 1972–1996 (Section 3).
  - No digging in 1975, 1986, 1988, 1997–1998, 2002–2003 or 2005–2015 (#73, #86, #88, #97, #98, #110, #111, #116, #117).
- **Shelters.**
  - Zinc sheets over the storeroom in 1967 (#16).
  - From 1968 Marinatos's Dexion-frame shelters with asbestos-cement roofing: c. 750 m² over Β–Γ in 1969 and c. 700 m² over Δ in 1970, joined into one shelter by 1971 (#27, #37, #48, #51, #99).
  - That shelter worked until the early 1990s (#100). It was replaced by N. Fintikakis's bioclimatic shelter: shafts dug 1999–2001, construction 2002–2005 (#102–#105, #110–#112).
- **Collapse and closure.** On 23 September 2005 part of the *new* shelter collapsed and a visitor died. Work stopped and the site stayed shut for seven years (#113, #117). The Society delivered the shelter complete on 12 April 2012 (#118); some press reports say the site reopened on 11 April (Section 5).
- **Pillar shafts.**
  - The new shelter's shafts were 3 × 4 m and 15–20 m deep to firm ground, at least 7 m into bedrock (#103, #106).
  - They are counted as 91 (Doumas 2016), "over 100" (2014) or "about 150" (2013) (#106–#108).
  - They revealed Early Cycladic rock-cut chambers and the Middle Cycladic town (#104). They are the main source for the earlier phases, but not the only one (Section 4, T8-5).
- **Visitor route.** Since 2012 walkways run around the perimeter of the excavated area, along existing routes; they are wheelchair-accessible and have bays for showcases (#120, #122, #123). No source opened describes, building by building, what can be seen from the route.
- **Wall paintings and finds, with the year found:**
  - Bronos 2 monkey head, 7 August 1968 (#30). "African" fragments, 1968 (#31). Room of the Monkeys (B6), 1969 (#39).
  - Spring (Lilies, Δ2) and the Boxers/Antelopes (B1), 1970 (#47, #50). The "Women" and the young priestess, 1971 (#51, #53).
  - Fisherman and miniature frieze (West House, room 5), 1972 (#64). Xeste 3 saffron gatherers, monkeys and reed bed, 1973 (#67). The four youths in Xeste 3, 1981 (#81).
  - Xeste 4 procession, 1974 and 1994–1996 (#69, #93, #96). Early Cycladic figurine hoard, 1992 (#91). Gold ibex, 1999 (#133).

## Section 4: Assumptions checked

- **T8-1: partly confirmed.**
  - Marinatos began the systematic dig in 1967 (#10). He died at the site in 1974 (#70).
  - The Society's board then gave the excavation to Doumas, whose first season was 1975 (#73). Doumas himself (#71) and a 2014 article (#121) say "since 1974".
  - Doumas is still named as director in Έργον 2020 (#130). The years 2021–2026 could not be verified (#131, #132).
- **T8-2: confirmed.** The dig began as separate trenches on both banks (#10–#20). Two roofed areas, Α and Β–Γ, had an unexcavated "bridge" between them (#34). The bridge was dug in 1970 to join them (#44, #45), and the shelters joined Δ to Α in 1971 (#51).
- **T8-3: partly confirmed.**
  - About 1 ha had been cleared by 1974 (#72); the site today is about 3 acres, or 1.2 ha converted (#123, #125). INFERENCE: most of the *footprint* was reached in the first eight seasons, 1967–1974.
  - But no building was finished by 1974 (#72). Most rooms, the House of the Ladies, the West House and Xeste 3 were completed later, and Curetes Street (#95) and the House of the Benches (#92, #127) are post-1974.
- **T8-4: partly confirmed.**
  - Most named buildings, streets and squares can be given a first-exposure year from the season reports.
  - Not the House of the Anchor, the "South Building", the "Porter's Lodge" or the cobbled side street. Xeste 5 and Building Gamma only to a span of years (#84, #34).
  - Dactyls Street, Double Horns Square and Millhouse Square only through the identifications in #61, #78 and #147 (C).
- **T8-5: partly confirmed.** The new shelter's shafts gave the fullest Early and Middle Cycladic evidence (#102–#106; Έργον 2019 on Nikolakopoulou's Middle Bronze Age volume). They were not the only source: earlier soundings at Sector Α in 1968 (#28), the Δ mill shaft in 1969 and the West House soundings of 1984–1990 (#89) also reached earlier levels.
- **T8-6: confirmed.** On 23 September 2005 part of the *new* shelter collapsed (#113). There was no excavation for the seven years of rebuilding (#117), and the Society delivered the shelter on 12 April 2012 (#118). The exact reopening day is disputed: 12 April (#118, #119), or 11 April in press reports seen only as snippets.

## Section 5: Debates

1. **When did the French dig?**
   - Marinatos (1967) and the 1967 Έργον put Mamet's work in 1867, "100 years ago" (#6, #22).
   - Tzachili and Karnava, citing Gorceix and Mamet's 1870 reports, date the digging to April–June 1870; Fouqué only detected the sites in 1867 (#1, #2).
   - The 1870 date has primary-report support. Marinatos may have taken Fouqué's year.
2. **Where did the dig "begin"?** Both are right and should not be confused.
   - The first trench, Bronos 1 (25 May 1967), lay in what became Complex Delta's area (#10, #44); Tzachili and Karnava also say the dig started "around Complex Delta" (#5).
   - But the first building actually excavated was the Pithoi Storeroom (Arvanitis 1, from 27 May) (#14, #16), and Delta itself was dug only from 1970.
3. **How many pillar shafts, and how deep?**
   - Counts: 35 by the end of 2000 (#103); 91 (Doumas 2016, #106); "over 100" (2014, #107); "about 150" (2013 web article, #108).
   - Depths: "15–20 m" (#103) and "at least 7 m into the bedrock" (#106) are compatible, measured from the surface and into the rock respectively.
   - The count differences may come from relocated shafts (#103) and from counting old-shelter shafts too; that is INFERENCE.
4. **When were the shafts dug?** 1999 to 2001 by the yearly reports (#102, #105). The 40-years foreword puts completion within 1997–2007 (#109). A Heidelberg glossary seen only as a search snippet gives 1999–2003 [snippet only, C].
5. **Reopening day.** 12 April 2012 per the Society (#118) and Archaeology magazine (#119). 11 April in press reports seen only as snippets (Reuters, Ta Nea) [snippet only, C].
6. **When did Doumas take over, and when was he first involved?**
   - Takeover: 1974 (#71, #121) or the 1975 season after the board's decision (#73).
   - First involvement: 1968 by his own account (#126), but the first staff list naming him is 1969, as curator (#32, #33).
7. **How many Marinatos seasons?** The Society twice speaks of "seven years" (#70, #98), yet reports exist for eight seasons, 1967–1974 (#10–#69).
8. **Labels that moved.**
   - "Δ" was first meant for a quarter west of Γ (1969) but went to the bridge (1970) (#36, #44).
   - The southern "Xeste E" (1971) is probably Xeste 3 (#58, #59).
   - The "northern extension of Telchines Street" (1977–1979) is probably Dactyls Street (#76–#78).
   - The 1971 "East Court" is probably Double Horns Square (#60, #61).
   - Builders should treat these identifications as C until checked against the published plans of 1969–1994 (#150–#153).
9. **What collapsed in 2005.** The Society says part of the *new* shelter (#113). A local site says about 5% of the roof (#115). Press accounts do not say whether the roof was finished. The accounts are compatible.
