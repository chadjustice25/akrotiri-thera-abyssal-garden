# T2b: the plan of the excavated town, the edges of the evidence, and the town's extent

Brief: `docs/research/prompts/T2b-town-plan-and-extent.md`. This lane follows T2 (`docs/research/claude/T2_Claude.md`, checked in `docs/research/verification/T2.md`) and cites it as T2#n. ## How this lane reads the grades and dates (read first)
- Grades follow T2: **A** is a remain of the last town seen or measured by the excavators; **B** is their debated interpretation, a geophysical or 19th-century observation not yet excavated, or an official summary; **C** is an estimate, a reconstruction, an analogy or a hypothesis. Rows about plans, licences and datasets carry **licence** or **data** instead of a grade.
- **INFERENCE** marks this lane's own measurement or reasoning. Measurements taken from the local plan give the pixel measurement and the scale used. Pixel figures refer to the file as it is (2,075 × 3,345 px), not to the 1,244 × 2,000 display size the brief mentions. Divide by 1.672 for display pixels.
- **Phase keys** are T2's. **K1** is final LC I, the town as buried (on the project's high chronology the living town runs c. 1645–1612 BCE and the year shown is 1615 BCE, `docs/WORLD_CLOCK.md`). **K2** is the LC I rebuilding after the earthquake of c. 1650 BCE (high). **K3** is Middle Cycladic. **K4** is Early Cycladic. **K5** is modern: a plan, a dataset, a shelter or a reconstruction. "Neolithic" is outside the keys (mid-5th millennium BC on Doumas's dates).
- **Names.** Every building, street, square, sector and terrace name in this report is a modern label given by the excavators (Marinatos, Doumas) or by map makers. "Telchinon Street" on the supplied plan is the same modern label as Telchines Street. No ancient name is known for any of them, or for the town.

## Section 1: Summary

**1. Which plan may be used, and on what terms**
- The supplied plan is byte-identical to Maximilian Dörrbecker's "Map Akrotiri 1600 BC-en.png" on Wikimedia Commons. The SHA-1 checksums match. It is dated 2007-05-20 and licensed **CC BY-SA 2.5 Generic, not 4.0**. (#1–#4)
- **Terms.** Under CC BY-SA 2.5 an adaptation must:
  - credit the author, the title and the URI, and say what was changed;
  - carry CC BY-SA 2.5 or a later version with the same elements (so CC BY-SA 4.0 is allowed), with a link to the licence.
  - A larger work that only *contains* the adaptation does not itself fall under the licence. (#7–#9)
- **Caveat 1: the plan is copied from others.** Dörrbecker says he digitised it from two maps a Wikipedia user supplied and from five websites. Its outline and wall shapes follow the Thera Foundation's 2005 web map almost line for line (INFERENCE). No terms could be found for that map. The CC licence covers Dörrbecker's own contribution, not necessarily the sources he traced. (#4, #10)
- **Caveat 2: Greek law.** Article 46 of Law 4858/2021 requires permission and a fee for "depictions" of monuments. Lane T6 owns that question. (#22, #23)
- **Other plans.**
  - Trinks et al. 2017 is CC BY 3.0 and contains a scaled laser-scan image of the whole excavated site. (#20)
  - OpenStreetMap (ODbL 1.0) maps only the modern shelter roof and its walkways, not the ancient walls. (#15, #16)
  - The "public domain" Greek plan on Commons appears to be traced from the excavators' plan. (#12)
  - The excavators' plans in Doumas 2016 are copyright. (T2#106)
- **Formal permission.** A request would go to the Ministry of Culture and Sports through the Ephorate of the Cyclades, for depictions. Unpublished plans and data would also need the Archaeological Society at Athens excavation, directed by Doumas, and TiMMA's director, as T2 found. Nothing found in this run changes T2's findings. (#22–#24)

**2. Scale and position**
- **No version has a scale bar or a north arrow, and there is no SVG.** (#5, #6)
- **Scale (INFERENCE): about 22 px per metre on the 2,075-px file (range 20.4–23.6, ±7%), or 13.2 px/m at display size.** Four checks agree (#31–#33):
  - West House Room 5 measures 89 × 81 px, against pier-and-window walls of 3.90 and 4.00 m (T2#42).
  - Xeste 4 is about 710 px long, against "over 30 m" (T2#24).
  - Xeste 3's plot is 283 m².
  - Distances match the excavators' topographical plan once that plan is scaled to the shelter in OpenStreetMap.
- **Accuracy.** Single buildings come out right to about 1 m. Distances across the whole site are good only to about ±5–10 m. Walls are drawn as uniform strokes, so street edges are uncertain. (#31, #35)
- **North (INFERENCE).** "Up" on the plan matches the excavators' plan, whose arrow points up, to within about 2°. The shelter's real outline lies about 5° anticlockwise of true north. So plan-up is true north to within about 5°. (#26, #32)
- **Position.**
  - The Ephorate gives the site as 36.3517952 N, 25.403447 E. (#25)
  - The shelter roof in OSM is a stepped polygon of about 1.22 ha. It is about 167 m north–south and 121 m east–west, widest (about 110 m) at the south. (#26)
  - The excavated walls span about 140 m north–south by 85 m east–west (INFERENCE); Doumas 1983 gives 90 × 120 m. (#29, #30)
  - Today's shore is about 200 m south of the shelter (INFERENCE from OSM). (#27)
- **Levels.**
  - Xeste 3 stands at about 19.50 m (T2#19).
  - A room at the east edge has its floor at 18.10–18.20 m above sea level. (#36)
  - The ground falls steeply east at Xeste 4, and the town sits on the crest of a north–south promontory. (#37–#39)
  - No measured gradient of any street was found.

**3. Where the evidence ends, direction by direction**
- **South.** Excavation stops at Xeste 3, Xeste 4 and the South Building.
  - Beyond: Neolithic sherds reach the ticket booths of the south gate, so there was land there (#63).
  - Palyvou's *indicative* prehistoric coastline runs about 35–55 m south of the site (INFERENCE from the published map, grade C) (#61).
  - The harbour has not been found (T1c#15–17).
- **West.** Excavation stops at the West House, the unexcavated House of the Anchor and Xeste 3.
  - Beyond: the indicative bay lies about 15–40 m to the west (C) (#61), and marine sand lies under the ash in the Mavro Rachidi valley (T1c#13, T1c#14).
  - Buried Bronze Age buildings stand on Kokkino Vouno, about 0.55–0.7 km to the west-southwest (B). Nothing shows whether they joined the town. (#75, #79)
- **East.** Excavation stops at Xeste 2, "Xeste 5" (known only as a label) and the east end of Xeste 4.
  - Beyond: the ground falls steeply (#38), and the indicative bay lies about 30–50 m east (C) (#61).
  - Zahn's 1899 excavation in the Potamos valley lies 500–600 m to the north-east and is "probably part of the town" (B/C) (#68, #69).
  - Graves are known in the Potamos valley (B, date not stated) (#77).
  - A rising "concave plateau" towards Potamos is read as houses collapsed beneath it (C) (#67).
- **North.** Excavation stops at Sector Alpha and the "Porter's Lodge". The Middle Cycladic town's northern limit was near Cenotaph Square (T2#15), so the last town had grown beyond it.
  - **No trench, core, survey or chance find north of the shelter was found in any source opened.**
  - Doumas's estimate runs the town "halfway" to the caldera (C) (#66).
- **Inside the dig.** A modern torrent cut north–south through the site, destroying the east wing of Complex Beta, much of Delta East and the east side of Dactyls Street (#53). The plan's empty strips there are gaps in the evidence, not open ground.
- **Everywhere.** No measured edge exists in any direction. No source opened mentions a town wall, either found or absent. (#70)

**4. Size, population and character**
- **Basis of the 20 ha estimate.** Doumas's tentative 20 ha (1983, as reported) rests on:
  - topography (sea to the south, Mesovouna to the west, Potamos to the east, north to halfway to the caldera);
  - Zahn's site;
  - the plateau argument. (#66–#69)
- **Basis of the 10 ha estimate.** Palyvou's 10 ha and 2,000 people (T2#2) rest on reasoning in Palyvou 2005 that could not be opened.
- **Population.** Sarpaki (1987) derives about 290–580 people for the excavated hectare, and 2,000–18,000 for 20 ha depending on the density assumed (C). (#73)
- **The dig's place in the town.** Doumas 2016 calls the excavated part the town's *centre* (#59). On Palyvou's coastline it is also the town's seaward *tip* (#61). Both hold: it is the southern, seaward end of a promontory town that probably ran north and spread east and west.

**5. Reconstructions of the whole town**
- Found:
  - Lloyd Townsend's painting for National Geographic (copyright, basis not stated) (#86);
  - the museum's scale model of the excavated part only (#87);
  - LBI ArchPro's scan, 7reasons' virtual tour and Trinks 2017's Triangle Square scenes (#20, #88, #89).
- None is a sourced reconstruction of the whole town. All are grade C.

## Section 4: Assumptions checked

- **T2b-1** (Dörrbecker, Commons, CC BY-SA, may be redrawn with credit and the same licence): **partly confirmed.** Author and Commons are right. The licence is CC BY-SA **2.5**, and an adaptation may carry 2.5 or a later version with the same elements, such as 4.0 (#1–#4, #7–#9). Two caveats: the map is traced from others' maps, closely following the Thera Foundation's 2005 web map, whose terms are unknown (#4, #10); and Greek law requires permission for depictions of monuments (#22, #23).
- **T2b-2** (a faithful simplification, scalable to within a metre or two): **partly confirmed.**
  - It is a simplification of the excavators' plan, by way of the Thera Foundation map, and agrees with Doumas's p. 23 plan to within about 2° and 5% (#10, #32).
  - Single buildings scale to about ±1 m: West House Room 5 and Xeste 4 check out (#31).
  - Whole-site distances are good only to about ±5–10 m, with a possible north–south stretch of up to 10% (#33).
  - Walls are uniform strokes (#34), the "Pylon" is misdrawn (#45), and there is no scale or north arrow (#5).
- **T2b-3** (about 1.2 ha, long north–south, Telchines Street the main street): **confirmed for area and shape; partly for the street.**
  - The roof is about 1.22 ha and the excavated walls about 140 × 85 m (#25, #26, #30, #72).
  - The Ephorate puts Telchines Street through the centre of the excavated part, and it is the longest named street on every plan (#25).
  - No source opened ranks the streets or gives widths.
- **T2b-4** (the excavated area is the town's seaward, southern part, with the town running inland and to both sides): **partly confirmed, with a debate.**
  - Palyvou's indicative coastline makes the dig the southern tip of a promontory between two bays (#60, #61).
  - Doumas's 20 ha estimate runs the town north to halfway to the caldera and east and west to Mesovouna and Potamos (#66).
  - Zahn's site 500–600 m north-east is "probably part of the town" (#68, #69).
  - But Doumas also calls the excavated part the town's **centre** (#59).
  - "To both sides" is limited by the two bays close to the site's south half (#61).
- **T2b-5** (no town wall found): **could not verify.** No source opened mentions a wall, found or absent (#70).
- **T2b-6** (nothing measured fixes the edge; 10 ha and 20 ha are indirect estimates): **confirmed.**
  - The 20 ha rests on topography, Zahn's site and the plateau-subsidence argument (#66–#68).
  - Sarpaki states there is no tangible evidence of where the town stopped (#70).
  - The basis of Palyvou's 10 ha could not be opened.

## Section 5: Debates

1. **Centre or edge?** Doumas (2016) calls the excavated sector the town's centre at its burial (#59), and places Xeste 3 and Xeste 4 at its south-west and south-east "edge" (#58). Palyvou's indicative coastline puts the dig at the sea end of a promontory (#61). The two readings are compatible if "centre" means the civic and economic core of a town that lay mostly to the north. The world can show both: a dense core at the tip, thinning inland.
2. **How big?**
   - **Doumas** gives 20 ha from the landscape (#66, #67).
   - **Palyvou** gives 10 ha, calls it the most modest estimate (T2#2), and in 1986 called 20 ha strikingly large (#66).
   - **Sarpaki** tested much larger hypothetical areas (172–274 ha), took in Balos and the Mavromati Quarry as suburbs, and rejected them as "extremely unlikely" (#73).
   - None rests on an excavated edge.
3. **Is Kokkino Vouno part of the town?**
   - Buildings, plaster and a crucible are attested there (#75, #79).
   - Vlachopoulos makes the hill the west arm of a harbour (#78), and others tie it to the frieze's hills (T1c#35).
   - Whether a continuous built area joined it to the excavated town is unknown.
4. **The number of pillar shafts:** 91 (Doumas 2016, T2#16) against "over 100" (Fintikakis and Doumas 2014, #55).
5. **Distance to today's shore:** about 200 m by OSM (#27) against about 125–145 m on the small-scale p. 22 map (#61). This is a measurement conflict, not a scholarly debate. OSM is georeferenced and preferred.

## Section 6: Game hooks (suggestions, not evidence)

- **One evidence mask, three tones.** Build the town from three layers that the world can show on demand:
  - **excavated** (the plan's walls, grade A geometry at ±1 m per building);
  - **known but unexcavated** (the House of the Anchor façade, the east side of Dactyls Street, the buildings south of Curetes Street, the torrent-eroded strips);
  - **reconstructed** (everything else).
  The dashed "limits" line is not the evidence boundary; the excavated walls are (#30).
- **Licence hygiene.** If the plan's geometry is digitised from Dörrbecker's map:
  - keep the traced data in its own file under CC BY-SA 4.0, crediting him with the Commons URL;
  - the rest of the MIT project can sit beside it as a collective work (#8, #9);
  - record it in the standard's dataset list.
  Alternatively, digitise from the CC BY 3.0 point-cloud figure (#20). Either way, T6 must settle art. 46 first (#22, #23).
- **Georeference.** Use the shelter roof as the control. Its stepped outline is in OSM, the Doumas p. 23 plan draws the same outline over the walls, and Dörrbecker's plan matches that plan within about 2°. Fit at 22 px/m, then rotate.
- **Edges.**
  - South: water within about 50 m of Xeste 3 and Xeste 4, shown as a liberty with the "indicative" coastline.
  - West and east: bays close to the southern half.
  - North: the town continues, thinning, as a grade-C reconstruction.
  - East: an outlying house cluster at Zahn's site 500–600 m north-east.
  - West: a few buildings on Kokkino Vouno, with a visible quarry face on its south slope (#79, #80).
- **No wall.** Do not build a town wall; none is reported (#70).
- **Telchines Street as the spine.** It is narrow (about 1.2–2.2 m on the plan) where it squeezes between Gamma and Beta, and opens into Triangle Square and the street by Delta 16. Mark the widths as liberties until Palyvou 2005 is read.
