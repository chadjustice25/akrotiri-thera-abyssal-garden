## How grades are applied in this lane (read first)
The A/B/C scale in `docs/HISTORICAL_STANDARD.md` §3 was written for objects. For landscape this lane applies it as follows:
- **A:** a physical observation on Thera in a pre-eruption (Late Cycladic I, or older and surviving into LC I) context, or a laboratory analysis of such material: the eruption deposits themselves, charcoal from LC I floors, the olive tree buried in the tephra, an excavated site.
- **B:** a present-day measurement of a landform that existed before the eruption, carried back to c. 1650 BCE unchanged; a physical finding on Thera whose interpretation is debated (for example the extent of the older caldera); a proxy record of the time from elsewhere in the southern Aegean carried over to Thera; or something shown in Theran art.
- **C:** a modern reconstruction or model (a digital elevation model of the lost island, a modelled sea level), an analogy from another period or place, later evidence, or a hypothesis.
- **Dates.** "ka" means thousands of years before present. This lane does not choose between the high (radiocarbon) and low (Egyptian-linked) chronologies; that belongs to W1/W2. Each source's date for the eruption is reported as the source gives it: "c. 3.6 ka" in the geological papers (about 1600 BCE); "probably at the end of the 17th century BC" (Greek Ministry of Culture); 1627–1600 BC at 95.4% (the olive-tree radiocarbon wiggle-match, high chronology); "c. 1400 B.C." (Heiken and McCoy 1984, superseded on both chronologies); LC I "1500–1450 BC" (Kufel, following an older low chronology).
- **Names.** Akrotiri, Thera, Therasia, Aspronisi, Profitis Ilias, Mesa Vouno, Kameni and the rest are modern place names. "Strongyli" and "Pre-Kameni" are modern scholarly labels. No Bronze Age name for the island or for any place on it is known.

## Section 1: Summary
- **There was already a caldera before the eruption, but it was smaller than today's and lay in the north.** The modern volcanological consensus is that a shallow, flooded caldera bay left by the Cape Riva eruption (c. 22 ka) occupied the northern part of today's caldera. The southern basin had been filled long before. (#1–#4, #7, #11)
- **The bay was not open to the southwest.** The newest reconstruction closes the rim from Thera through Aspronisi to Therasia, with at most a low, narrow strait to the north. The wide northwest channel between Thera and Therasia was cut after the eruption, when the sea flooded in. (#9–#11)
- **The bay held brackish to marine water with stromatolites,** with volcanic and hydrothermal input, in shallow water. Its depth has not been published as a number. (#7, #12)
- **An island stood in the bay: "Pre-Kameni".** It was built of black glassy andesite, began growing c. 20.2 ± 1.0 ka, and had a volume of about 2.2–2.5 km³ (another estimate gives 1.6–3.0 km³). Its height and footprint are not published. The reconstruction models it as a low dome. (#8, #13–#15)
- **The eruption destroyed 9.1–17.1 km³ of the ring island** besides Pre-Kameni. Up to 3 km³ more may have sunk during the collapse. This is the land that has to be put back, mostly on the caldera side. (#14)
- **Today's caldera is 10 × 7 km, its walls about 400 m high, and its floor down to about 390 m.** These are post-eruption numbers and set the maximum envelope, not the 1650 BCE shape. Part of the northern cliff line already existed before the eruption: Minoan pumice is plastered on the cliff at Fira harbour at about 140 m elevation. (#17–#20)
- **The pumice and ash to strip off are thick.** The eruption's units are up to 5.5 m, 12 m, 55 m and 80 m thick (averages about 2, 5 and 25 m on land). The pre-eruption valley floor at Mavrorachidi, beside Akrotiri, lies about 25 m below today's surface. (#24, #37)
- **The Akrotiri town sat on gently rising ground near the south coast,** around a small hill at about 20–25 m above sea level, with sheltered bays nearby. The harbour has not been found. Coring and geophysics target three small coastal valleys on either side of the town. (#32, #35–#37)
- **Thera was busy.** About 16 places on Thera, Therasia and Aspronisi have produced Late Cycladic material, most of them on the Akrotiri peninsula, plus Balos on the caldera edge, Ftellos near Fira and a quarry near Oia. (#38–#43)
- **The climate was Mediterranean, with more trees than today.** Akrotiri's charcoal shows open oak woodland, pine and cultivated olive, and points to a relatively moist and cool climate. Crete's pollen shows no major Holocene climate shift. The summer north wind (Etesian) is a modern climatology carried back, not a measured fact for 1650 BCE. (#46–#50)
- **Sea level was probably lower than today, perhaps by more than T1-4 assumes.** In the northern and central Cyclades the sea stood at 3.6 ± 0.2 m below present from c. 2000 BC to c. 30 BC. Santorini's own vertical movement could not be verified. (#51, #52)
- **The Kameni islands did not exist.** They grew after the Minoan eruption, and the first recorded emergence above the sea was in 197–96 BC (Strabo). They cover about 3.9 km² today and must be removed. (#26–#31)

## Section 4: Assumptions checked
- **T1-1** (ring island around a largely flooded older caldera, open on at least one side): **partly confirmed.** The caldera existed and was flooded or lagoonal (#1–#4, #7, #12). It was smaller than today's and lay in the north (#2, #3, #11). Its opening to the sea is only possible, as a low, narrow northern strait (#11), and the SW rim was closed (#11). "Largely" overstates it.
- **T1-2** (an island inside the caldera): **confirmed in substance** (#8, #13). Its size is known only as a volume (#13–#15), and its height and footprint could not be verified.
- **T1-3** (Profitis Ilias and the south near Akrotiri had today's shape, minus the ash): **partly confirmed.** The basement massif is unchanged (#21). But the caldera rim and cliffs changed (#14, #20, #39), valleys near Akrotiri are filled by about 25 m (#37), and the tephra cover is tens of metres in places (#24). The south coast's pre-eruption line could not be verified.
- **T1-4** (sea level within about 1–2 m of today's): **contradicted for the region; could not verify for Thera.** The central Cyclades stood at −3.6 ± 0.2 m (#51, #52). Santorini's own vertical movement could not be verified.
- **T1-5** (a coastal town with a harbour on the south coast): **partly confirmed.** The town was coastal, with anchorages and sheltered bays (#32, #35, #37). The harbour itself has not been found (#37), and the location between Mesa Vouno and Mavrorachidi is a hypothesis (#36).
- **T1-6** (Mediterranean, much like today, perhaps a little wetter): **partly confirmed.** The climate was Mediterranean, with more woodland and "relatively moist and cool" conditions from the Akrotiri charcoal (#46, #47). Crete shows no major Holocene change (#48). A rejected preprint suggests regional drying after 3.7 ka (#49).
- **T1-7** (the Kameni islands did not exist; they formed from 197 BCE): **confirmed in substance** (#27–#31). The 197–96 BC date rests on Strabo (#27). When submarine growth began after the eruption could not be verified.

## Section 5: Debates
1. **Where was the old caldera?**
   - South: Heiken and McCoy (1984) put a pre-existing caldera in the south of the present caldera, formed by the 172 ka Lower Pumice eruption (#5, #6).
   - North: Druitt and Francaviglia (1992) put the 22 ka caldera across much of the northern basin, from Minoan pumice adhering to the modern cliff (#5, #19). Cosmogenic dating of the cliffs supports the northern view (#4), and Karátson et al. (2018, 2020) restrict the bay to the north, with the southern basin long filled (#2, #3).
   - Status: the current volcanological position is the northern caldera. Druitt and Francaviglia's own text could not be opened.
2. **Open harbour, semi-closed bay or lagoon?**
   - Karátson et al. (2018) describe a "semi-closed caldera harbour" with a possible low northern strait and a closed SW rim (#1, #11).
   - Nomikou et al. (2016) call it lagoonal, with marine and brackish fauna, and give no pre-eruption channel (#7, #9).
   - Older archaeological syntheses put a western "entrance to the caldera" by Aspronisi, with an LC settlement there (#38, citing Forsyth 1997). That conflicts with a closed SW rim.
   - Status: unresolved. For the build, a narrow northern opening is the best-supported option, and is a liberty in its width.
3. **A central cone or a caldera with an island?** Archaeologists have generally favoured an exploded central cone, volcanologists a pre-existing caldera with Pre-Kameni (#16). Karátson et al. (2020) read the Flotilla Fresco as possibly showing the caldera landscape. That reading is grade B at best (shown is not present) and belongs with the T-lane on wall paintings.
4. **Pre-Kameni and Kameni volumes.** For Pre-Kameni, 2.2–2.5 km³ (2018) against 1.6–3.0 km³ (2020) (#13, #14). For Kameni today, 3.2 km³ (#29) against 4.85 ± 0.7 km³ (#28). The differences are probably in method (subaerial against total products); this is unverified.
5. **Sea level.** T1-4's 1–2 m is not supported regionally. Kolaiti and Mourtzas give −3.6 m for the central Cyclades across the whole second and first millennia BC, with a tectonic component of 1–1.2 m beyond the glacio-isostatic model (#51, #52). Santorini sits on its own volcano-tectonic zone, and no Santorini-specific Bronze Age sea-level marker was found.
6. **Climate.** The Akrotiri charcoal (#46) reads moist and cool, while the Peloponnese stalagmite preprint (#49) shows drying from 3.7 ka. NW Crete's pollen shows no major change (#48). The charcoal evidence is local and direct but interpretive. The speleothem preprint was not accepted by its journal.
7. **The eruption's date.** It is given as 1627–1600 BC by the olive-tree wiggle-match (#25), "end of the 17th century BC" by the Ministry (#33), and LC I as late as 1500–1450 BC in Kufel's older table (#44). Choosing between them belongs to W1/W2 and `docs/WORLD_CLOCK.md`, not this lane.
