## Section 1: Summary

1. **There is still no agreed eruption date.** Under IntCal20 the radiocarbon range runs from the late 17th century through the whole 16th century BCE. Manning 2022 gives 1609–1560 BCE (95.4%) and Manning 2024 favours c. 1611 BCE. Pearson et al. 2023 give the Therasia shrub 1610–1510 BCE (95.4%) and favour the mid-16th century (#12, #13, #14, #62).
2. **The Egyptian-linked ("low") position puts the eruption c. 1525–1490 BCE.** It rests on Theran pumice, which appears in Egyptian and Levantine contexts only from the Thutmosid period, and on White Slip I pottery (#3, #20, #21, #22). Both kinds of evidence give only a latest possible date (a terminus ante quem) (#23, #44).
3. **The two camps are now closer: the dispute is about 50 years wide, not 100.** One reading is c. 1600–1611 BCE and the other is c. 1560 BCE or later (#13, #14, #62, #68). Bruins & van der Plicht 2025 date British Museum Ahmose material and conclude that the eruption predates Ahmose (#15, #16).
4. **The 1628 BCE ice-core and tree-ring signal is the Alaskan eruption Aniakchak II, not Thera.** Its glass has been chemically matched. **No ice-core tephra has been matched to Thera** (#17, #18). The ice-core candidates for Thera are 1611, 1561/1558/1555 and c. 1538 BCE. The authors lean towards 1561 BCE (#18, #81).
5. **Whichever chronology is used, Akrotiri's final phase (late LM IA / LC I) falls inside the 1800–1500 BCE window.** High: LM IA c. 1700/1675–1625/1600 BCE. Low: LM IA c. 1600–1480 BCE (#26, #27).
6. **The earthquake came before the eruption, but the interval is disputed.** Doumas once suggested a few months. Marthari's pottery comparison suggests years, perhaps two or three decades. The first pumice fall lay exposed for an estimated 2–24 months (#29, #30).
7. **Egypt c. 1650 BCE, in the UEE/Schneider chronology:** the 15th ("Hyksos") Dynasty ruled at Avaris (1640–1532) and the 17th Dynasty ruled at Thebes (1690–1548). The late 13th Dynasty had just ended (1809–1656). Ahmose reigned 1548–1523 (#34). The Avaris kings probably ruled the eastern Delta directly, but how far they controlled Middle Egypt is unclear (#38).
8. **Mesopotamia and Anatolia (hMC):** Hammurabi reigned 1792–1750 BCE. Babylon fell to Mursili I in 1595 BCE (1587 lMC). Hattusili I reigned conventionally c. 1650–1620 BCE. New radiocarbon evidence from Kültepe and Zincirli supports only the Middle Chronology (#45, #47, #48, #49, #50, #54).
9. **The northern Levant's destruction horizon falls in the later 17th century BCE.** It includes Alalakh VII, Ebla IIIB2, Tilmen and Zincirli, which radiocarbon dates to c. 1632–1610 BCE (56.2%). It is linked to Hattusili I's campaigns (#49, #51).
10. **Avaris c. 1650 BCE** was a town of about 250 ha with a Levantine population and imports from Syria, Palestine and Cyprus. Near the end of the Hyksos period a citadel was built on the Pelusiac branch of the Nile (#55, #56, #57). **Byblos** was an autonomous city of about 5 ha. Its rulers used the Egyptian title *haty-a*, and it controlled the cedar trade from a newly located harbour (#64, #65, #66).
11. **The Minoan paintings at Tell el-Dab'a belong to the Thutmosid palaces (c. 1479–1450 BCE), not to the Hyksos period** (#58). Radiocarbon dates the Aegean-style paintings at Tel Kabri a century or more earlier than expected (#61).
12. **The Khyan lid from Knossos could not be checked against an opened primary source.** Khyan's own position in the 15th Dynasty is debated (#37, Section 11).
13. **Calendars.** Egypt counted regnal years and a 365-day civil year of three seasons and five extra days (#40, #41, #42). Babylonia used lunar months, year names and intercalation by royal letter, which is attested under Hammurabi (#46, #71, #72). No Aegean calendar of the window is known (#75).
14. **Shared datable events in the window** include frost rings and sulfate in 1656/1654/1653 BCE, Aniakchak II in 1628/1627 BCE, and sulfate peaks in 1611, 1586, 1561, 1551 and c. 1538 BCE. The Porsuk tree-ring anomaly (1681–1673 BCE) is not Thera (#17, #79, #80, #81).
15. **The Greek Ministry of Culture's page for the Museum of Prehistoric Thera dates the burial of the town "probably at the end of the 17th century BC"** (#82).

Where the high and low positions stand now: the radiocarbon camp has split. One group still reads c. 1611–1590 BCE under IntCal20 (Manning 2022, 2024). ## Section 9: Byblos and Avaris c. 1650 BCE

**Avaris (Tell el-Dab'a).** The identification rests on a sealing of a "mayor of Avaris" (#59). By the Hyksos period (c. 1640–1530 BC on the project's dates) the town covered about 250 ha (#55). Bietak calls it the largest city of its time in Egypt and the eastern Mediterranean (#59). Its population had been partly Levantine since the late 12th Dynasty. It had a 13th-Dynasty palatial quarter for officials of Asiatic origin and Near Eastern-type temples (#57). It stood on the Pelusiac branch of the Nile and was a seaport (#59). It imported Syro-Palestinian amphorae, and Cypriot pottery imports rose after c. 1650 (#55). Near the end of the period a large citadel was built on its western edge (#56). Rulers in the UEE/Schneider chronology: the 15th Dynasty, 1640–1532 (Salitis, Sheshi, Khian, Apep, Khamudi) (#34). The Avaris kings probably ruled the eastern Delta directly (#38). Ahmose took the city c. 1532–1530 BC, apparently without a large destruction layer (#58, #89).

**Byblos.** An old, small city of about 5 ha (#64). Its importance came from the cedar of Mount Lebanon and a harbour recently located below the South Gate (#66). Its rulers were autonomous but used the Egyptian title *haty-a*, and a Mari text names a ruler of Byblos, Yantin-Hammu (#64, #65). The Obelisk Temple and the Baalat-Gebal temple were the main sanctuaries; both had non-Egyptian plans (Charaf 2024, the source of #64–#67) [verify]. Whether the royal tombs of Abishemu and Abishemu-Abi date to the late 19th century or to the Hyksos period is debated (#67). For the Hyksos period (1650–1550 BC), Charaf describes Byblos as still importing looted Middle Kingdom objects (#67). Who ruled Byblos in 1650 BCE could not be verified from an opened source.

## Section 10: Assumptions checked

- **X1-1 (eruption c. 1630–1500 BCE; radiocarbon later 17th c., archaeology c. 1530–1500):** partly confirmed. The radiocarbon ranges now run from 1660–1613 (Manning 2006, IntCal04) to 1610–1510 BCE (Pearson 2023, IntCal20), so they extend above 1630 and into the 16th century. The archaeological range cited is 1525–1490 BC (#3, #6, #12, #14, #20).
- **X1-2 (tree-ring calibration narrows the gap and points to the 16th c.):** partly confirmed. The annual data did move the calibration (#7, #8, #9), and Pearson et al. and Graziadio favour the mid-16th century (#12, #13, #68). Manning 2022/2024 still gets c. 1611–1589 BCE from IntCal20 (#14, #62). The gap is now about 50 years (#62).
- **X1-3 (LC I / LM IA falls inside 1800–1500 under either chronology):** confirmed (#26, #27).
- **X1-4 (LM IA lasted decades; earthquake months to a few years before):** partly contradicted. LM IA lasted at least 75 years in both tables (#26, #27). On the interval, Doumas suggested months, but Marthari argues years, perhaps two or three decades, and Rutter accepts years to decades (#29).
- **X1-5 (no ice-core tephra matched to Thera):** confirmed (#17, #18).
- **X1-6 (c. 1650 Egypt in the SIP, 15th Dynasty ruling the north from Avaris):** confirmed under UEE/Schneider (#34, #35), with nuance: direct Hyksos rule was probably limited to the eastern Delta (#38).
- **X1-7 (Hammurabi 1792–1750; Babylon falls 1595, Middle Chronology):** confirmed (#45, #48). Note the low-Middle alternative of 1587 (#48).
- **X1-8 (Byblos c. 1650 a Middle Bronze Age city-state with close ties to Egypt):** confirmed in general (#64, #65, #66, #67). No named ruler for 1650 could be verified.
- **X1-9 (Tell el-Dab'a paintings early 18th Dynasty, not Hyksos):** confirmed: Thutmose III phase C/3 (#58).
- **X1-10 (Khyan lid ties a 15th-Dynasty king to MM III Crete):** could not verify from an opened primary source. Khyan's own position is debated (#37).
- **X1-11 (Old Hittite kingdom founded by Hattusili I mid-17th c., Middle Chronology):** partly confirmed. c. 1650–1620 BC is a conventional count back from 1595 (#48). The Zincirli destruction c. 1632–1610 BC (56.2%) fits his reign (#49). Who founded the kingdom is not addressed in the opened source.
- **X1-12 (365-day civil calendar, drifting about one day in four years):** partly confirmed. The 365 days and five epagomenal days are confirmed (#40, #41). The drift rate is INFERENCE, not stated in an opened source.
- **X1-13 (no Bronze Age calendar from Crete or the Cyclades reconstructed):** consistent with everything found. No such reconstruction was located, and Linear B evidence is later (#75). The absence itself could not be verified from a source that states it.
- **X1-14 (no well-dated climate event in the window other than the eruption is securely tied to Thera):** confirmed. The 1628/1627, 1645 ± 7 and Porsuk signals have all been detached from Thera (#17, #19, #79, #80).

## Section 11: Debates and could not verify

**Debates**
1. **Eruption date.**
   - High, late 17th c.: Friedrich et al. 2006 and 2014 reply (#1, #4); Manning et al. 2006, 2014 (#5, #24); Manning 2022, 2024 (#14, #62).
   - Radiocarbon read as mid-16th c.: Pearson et al. 2018, 2022, 2023 (#8, #18, #13); Ehrlich et al. 2021 (#11); Graziadio 2024 (#68).
   - Low, c. 1525–1490: Cherubini et al. 2014 (#3); Bietak 2014 (#20, #21, #22, #88).
   - Eruption before Ahmose, but Ahmose dated low: Bruins & van der Plicht 2025 (#15).
2. **The olive branch.** Cherubini et al. say olive rings cannot be counted and the branch may have been dead (#3). Friedrich et al. reply that an ordered sequence still gives a 17th-century date (#4). Ehrlich et al. confirmed annual growth in a modern olive but found anomalies (#11). Pearson et al. 2023 report that the Therasia shrub is slightly younger (#12).
3. **The Tell el-Dab'a radiocarbon offset.** Bietak reads the 100–120-year offset as a radiocarbon problem near the sea (#22, #88). Manning and colleagues read the same data as raising the end of the Middle Bronze Age to c. 1600 (#53).
4. **White Slip I and the pumice.** Bietak treats both as dating evidence (#20, #21). Höflmayer, cited by Friedrich, treats them as giving only a latest possible date (#23). Popko agrees that the pumice gives only a terminus ante quem (#44).
5. **The Tempest Stela.** Ritner & Moeller link it to Thera (#83); Popko calls the interpretation controversial (#44).
6. **Byblos royal tombs.** Late 19th century (conventional) or SIP/Hyksos (Kopetzky) (#67).
7. **Dynasty 16.** UEE/Schneider lists minor Hyksos rulers (#34); Ilin-Tomich's text treats Dynasty 16 as Theban (#39).
8. **The Kabri paintings.** The early radiocarbon dates would make Levantine Aegean-style painting earlier than Akrotiri's. The authors themselves caution that the dates are unconfirmed (#61).
9. **Aniakchak II.** The ice-core date is 1628 BCE (#17), but a peat date of 1636–1446 cal BC hints that it may be younger (#85).
10. **LM IB length.** The high chronology implies LM IB lasted 125–175 years, which many find unlikely (#86).
