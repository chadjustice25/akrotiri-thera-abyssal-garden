## How to read the grades and dates
- **Grades** follow `docs/HISTORICAL_STANDARD.md` §3. **A:** found on Thera (or Therasia) in a pre-eruption (Late Cycladic I) context, or a laboratory analysis of such material. **B:** shown in Theran art (shown is not present); found elsewhere in the Aegean and carried over; or found on Thera but its interpretation is debated. **C:** a modern observation carried back, an analogy, a modern reconstruction or a hypothesis.
- **A split grade** ("A find / B reading") means the object is grade A but what it is taken to show is grade B.
- **Phase codes** used in the "Phase and date" column. Absolute dates come from X1 and are not chosen here (that is `docs/WORLD_CLOCK.md`'s job):
  - **LCI-end**: the final occupation and destruction horizon of Akrotiri, Late Cycladic I = Late Minoan IA (late). Eruption under the **high (radiocarbon) chronology** c. 1611–1600 BCE (Manning, X1 #13–#14); under the **low (Egyptian-linked) chronology** c. 1560–1500 BCE (X1 #2, #12). Distance from the eruption: weeks to a few decades; the seismic destruction came first and its interval to the eruption is disputed (X1 #29–#30).
  - **LCI**: Late Cycladic I in general. High: LM IA c. 1700/1675–1625/1600 BCE; low: c. 1600–1480 BCE (X1 #26–#27). From 0 to roughly a century before the eruption. The wall paintings are LCI; a source's own date is added where it gives one.
  - **ERUPT**: the eruption deposits themselves, with the source's date and calibration curve.
  - **EC–LCI**: Early Cycladic to Late Cycladic I (3rd millennium BCE to the eruption; the source gives no absolute range), so up to c. 1,400 years before the eruption. INFERENCE for the span.
  - **MC**: Middle Cycladic, before LC I; the source gives no absolute range.
  - **MOD**: a present-day observation, carried back; always grade C.
- **No ancient word or name** for any plant or animal of Thera is known from Linear A, which is undeciphered. All names here are modern Latin or English.

## Section 1: Summary

1. **The countryside was olive, pulses, barley and phrygana.** The last kitchen before the eruption burned mostly olive wood and olive-pressing waste, then tamarisk, pine and deciduous oak, with pear/hawthorn and aromatic low shrubs (thyme, savory, lavender, thorny burnet, Thymelaea). The authors place phrygana and maquis close to the town (#4–#8).
2. **Crops found, not just painted:** hulled six-row and two-row barley, einkorn and a free-threshing wheat; lentil, pea, chickpea, bitter vetch, faba bean (cf.), several *Lathyrus* (including *L. clymenum*, identified as the same variety as the pulse still grown for food on Thera); grape (pips and stalks), olive (stones, whole fruit), fig (charred and mineralised), flax, coriander, lupin (#1–#5).
3. **Olive is the best-attested plant on the island.** An olive tree was buried standing in the pumice on the caldera rim, crown in life position (#12). An olive shrub was charred between terrace stones on Therasia at about 196 m above sea level (#13). Olive is about half of identified Early Bronze Age charcoal [verify] (#11). Almond, ash, cypress and chestnut (the last two brought in) also appear in the charcoal (#10).
4. **There were more trees than today**, with open oak woodland, but by LC I the landscape was managed, wood may have been short, and dung was burned as fuel (#8–#10). (T1 #46–#47 hold the climate reading.)
5. **Livestock: sheep and goats, then pigs, cattle few.** In the one fully published bone sample opened (1,143 fragments from a kitchen), sheep/goat are 92.85% of fragments, pig 5.59%, cattle 1.31%, hare 0.17%, red deer one antler (#37). A whole pig was found in a room of Complex Delta [verify] (#41). The settlement-wide figures (Trantalidou 2000; Gamble 1979) could not be opened.
6. **Deer did not live on Thera.** Red-deer antlers came in from the mainland; a deposit of hundreds of pairs of horns and antlers lay under the pumice near Xeste 3 [verify] (#39, #44).
7. **The sea was eaten small, with exceptions.** Most fish bones are tiny sea breams and picarels; a fish paste, a split dried dentex, a pithos of red porgy and two slices of a c. 1 m tuna (probably bluefin) in a baking tray are the striking finds (#45–#48). Murex (mostly *Hexaplex trunculus*) was crushed and reused in floors; lumps of true murex purple pigment were found, but no dye workshop (#49–#51).
8. **The monkeys are art only (T3-1 confirmed).** Monkeys were not native to the Aegean and no bone of one has been found, even of a kept animal (#55). Their species is a live debate: African vervet/grivet (*Cercopithecus aethiops* group) versus South Asian grey langur (#56–#60).
9. **The "antelopes" are art only.** They have been read as East African oryx (*Oryx beisa*) or as gazelles (Dorcas, Grant's or Soemmerring's). No antelope or gazelle bone is known from the Aegean except one horn on Samos (#61–#62).
10. **Saffron is painted, not found (T3-2 partly).** No crocus flower, bulb or saffron has been recovered at Akrotiri. Painted crocuses are purple with red stigmas; the species (wild *C. cartwrightianus* or cultivated *C. sativus*) is debated. Saffron crocuses flower in **autumn**, for a few days (#16–#26).
11. **The Spring Fresco's season is disputed (T3-7 partly).** The museum reads spring, March–April. Botanists note the lily form flowers May–July. The red lilies mix features of the white Madonna lily and the red *L. chalcedonicum*, a humid-mountain plant unlikely on Thera (INFERENCE) (#27–#30). The "lilies" of the House of the Ladies are probably sea daffodils, a beach plant that flowers July–September (#31–#32).
12. **The kitchen was abandoned mid-meal.** Tuna slices were left cooking. Few animal remains of any kind were left in the town because people, and presumably their animals, had gone (INFERENCE) (#46, #53).

## Section 4: Assumptions checked

- **T3-1** (monkeys did not live wild on Thera): **confirmed.** Monkeys were not native to the Aegean, and no remains are known even of kept animals (#55); the bone samples opened contain none (#37). Painted monkeys are blue-grey and of debated species (#54, #56–#60).
- **T3-2** (saffron crocus grew on Thera and saffron was gathered there): **partly confirmed, as art only.** Crocus gathering is painted in Xeste 3 (#18), and murex purple was used for the painted petals (#26). No crocus flower, bulb or saffron has been found (#16, #17). Wild saffron is gathered on Santorini today, a grade-C analogy (#23). Species and cultivation are debated (#21, #24).
- **T3-3** (the red lilies are *Lilium chalcedonicum*, which may not have grown on Thera): **partly confirmed.** The lilies are red like *L. chalcedonicum*, but their buds and leaves are those of white *L. candidum*; they may be a blend (#29). *L. chalcedonicum* is a plant of humid wooded mountains (#30), so its growing on Thera is unlikely (INFERENCE; could not verify its modern Cycladic distribution).
- **T3-4** (olive, vine and fig grown on Thera before the eruption): **confirmed for olive; vine and fig found, growing inferred.** Olive grew there: a tree was buried standing (#12), a shrub stood among terraces on Therasia (#13), and olive wood dominates the charcoal (#5, #6, #11). Grape pips and stalks and fig fruit were found (#1, #4). That vine and fig grew on the island is INFERENCE (stalks suggest local processing). No vine or fig wood was reported in the charcoal opened.
- **T3-5** (sheep and goats were the main livestock; cattle were few): **confirmed for the one sample opened.** In the kitchen sample, caprines are 92.85%, pig 5.59% and cattle 1.31% (#37). The settlement-wide counts (Trantalidou 2000; Gamble 1979) could not be opened.
- **T3-6** (pre-eruption Thera had more trees than today): **confirmed** (#9; T1 #46–#47). The charcoal also shows a managed landscape and pressure on wood: dung and crop waste were burned as fuel (#8, #10).
- **T3-7** (swallows and lilies show spring; swallows reach the Aegean in March–April): **partly confirmed.** The museum reads spring, March–April (#28). Botanists read the lilies as early summer, May–July (#29, #30), and Xeste 3's crocuses are an autumn plant (#22). Barn swallows reach NW Croatia about 31 March (#70); an earlier arrival in the southern Aegean is likely but could not be verified for Greece.

## Section 5: Debates

1. **Which monkeys?** (a) African green monkeys (*Cercopithecus aethiops*/*sabaeus*, vervet/grivet): Marinatos; Georma; Urbani and Youlatos; Pruetz and Greenlaw (#56, #59, #60). (b) South Asian grey langur (*Semnopithecus*), from tail carriage, with knowledge passing through Mesopotamia: Pareja et al. 2019, 2020 (#57–#59). Both sides agree the painted animals are blue-grey and that the paintings are art, not field studies. The live question for the builder is only the reference species. Neither side claims monkeys lived on Thera.
2. **Oryx or gazelle?** Marinatos: *Oryx beisa*. Trantalidou: *Gazella dorcas* or *G. granti*. Masseti: Grant's or Soemmerring's gazelle, possibly from a live animal brought through Egypt. Georma notes artistic hybridism with the wild goat (#61, #62). The gold figurine is similarly read as ibex (Doumas) or gazelle/saiga (Masseti) (#63).
3. **Which crocus, wild or cultivated?** *C. sativus* (cultivation) against *C. cartwrightianus* (wild, gathered among rocks), with *C. oreocreticus* also proposed. Day doubts the Minoans drew the distinction. Kazemi-Shahandashti et al. read the frescoes as the first signs of saffron cultivation but admit no plant remains confirm it (#21, #24).
4. **What season does the Spring Fresco show?** Spring, March–April: the National Archaeological Museum (#28). Early summer, from the lilies: Kandeler and Ullrich (#29). Day warns that the crocus scenes were wrongly read as spring or summer, since saffron crocus flowers in autumn (#22). The weevils suggest the eruption itself fell in summer (#14).
5. **Sea daffodil or papyrus** (House of the Ladies)? Sea daffodil: Marinatos 1976, Baumann 2006 (#31). Papyrus: Warren 1976, who argues papyrus could have reached the Aegean (#34). Baumann objects that papyrus, a Nile-marsh plant and an Egyptian state symbol, is unlikely.
6. **Where is the Miniature Fresco's river?** North Africa or the Near East (Rackham, from the date palms) or Crete (Warren, from Cretan palm-fringed rivers) (#33).
7. **Food waste or dye works?** The crushed murex is read as food debris, or as recycled waste from a purple-dye industry outside the excavated area; Sotiropoulou et al. favour a Theran dye industry (#49–#51).
8. **Bovines or goats in B6?** The older reading is "Bovines and Crocuses" (Day, citing Sarpaki: animals kept from browsing the flowers); Georma's new reconstruction makes them goats (#64).
9. **Apiary?** Papageorgiou reads the Miniature Frieze structure as terraced beehives; earlier readings differ (#69). No excavated hives or wax residues from Akrotiri were found in the sources opened.
10. **Migratory fish: painted but rare in bones.** The Fisherman paintings show dolphinfish and small tunas (#65), but among several thousand fish bones only the kitchen tuna is a migratory scombrid (#46); the everyday catch was small inshore fish (#45).

## Section 6: Game hooks (suggestions only, not evidence)

These are design suggestions. Each would need its own liberty row (standard §6) where it goes beyond the claims.

- **Two layers of nature.** Build the island's living things only from grade-A finds (olive, pulses, barley, phrygana shrubs, sheep, goats, pigs, a few cattle, hare, mice, dogs, inshore fish, murex). Keep the painted ones (monkeys, antelopes, griffins, lilies, papyrus, palms) inside the paintings on the walls. A viewer who looks for a monkey outdoors finds none: the "shown is not present" law, made playable.
- **Ground cover from the kitchen.** Terraced olive groves near the town, grading upslope into low grey-green phrygana (thyme, savory, lavender, thorny burnet, *Thymelaea*, asphodel), with scattered pine, oak and tamarisk in gullies and near the shore (#6–#7, #9–#10, #13). Tamarisk near the shore is an INFERENCE from its usual habitat, not a source.
- **Sea life by depth.** Shoals of small sea breams and picarels inshore; a rare tuna or dolphinfish offshore; common dolphins in groups of about 4–9 (#45, #46, #65, #72).
- **The interrupted meal.** A cold hearth with a baking tray of tuna, cracked barley and split pulses in the store, and olive-pit fuel: a quiet, evidenced way to show the town was abandoned (#2, #4, #5, #46).
- **Season.** Because the eruption's season is disputed (spring from the fresco's reading, summer from the weevils), pick one season as a liberty and say so in the world. Summer would match the weevil evidence (#14) and the dry phrygana.
- **Colour palette.** Painted crocus purple came from murex (#26); dried sea-daffodil blue and lily red are painters' choices, not plant colours (#27, #31).
