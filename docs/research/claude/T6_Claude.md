## Read first: how this lane applies grades, dates and names
- **Grades.** A pigment identified by laboratory analysis of an Akrotiri painting is **A**. What a painting *shows* (a monkey, a blue sky, a red man) is **B** at most (Law 2 of the standard): it tells us how Therans painted, not what was there. A painting's location, size and subject, recorded by the excavators, is **A** for the painting as an object. Evidence from Kea, Crete or Tell el-Dab'a carried over to Thera is **B**. Colour values measured on modern geological samples from Anafi, a putative but unproven pigment source, are **C**. Law and museum terms are graded **"n/a (law)"**: the A/B/C scale is for the past, not for present-day rules.
- **Dates.** All the paintings in this lane were on the walls, or lying fallen inside the rooms, when the town was buried. The sources call this level the "Volcanic Destruction Level" (VDL) of **Late Cycladic I (LC I), equivalent to Late Minoan IA (LM IA)**. They give different absolute dates and none names its chronology explicitly: the Ephorate of Antiquities of the Cyclades says the burial "probably" came at the end of the 17th century BC (this matches the high, radiocarbon chronology); the National Archaeological Museum says c. 1600 BC / 16th century BC (closer to the low, Egyptian-linked chronology); Profi et al. (1977) say 1600–1500 BC (an older low chronology); Hejl and Tippelt (2005) think the paintings were "probably" made in the mid-17th century BC (their inference). This lane does not choose; W1/W2 and `docs/WORLD_CLOCK.md` do. "Distance from the eruption" is 0 for everything found in the VDL; how long before the eruption each painting was *made* is not known for any of them (see #66 and Section 4, T6-6).
- **Names.** "West House", "Xeste 3", "House of the Ladies", "Porter's Lodge", "Building Beta", "Sector/Complex Delta" and the titles of the paintings ("Fisherman", "Potnia", "Saffron Gatherers", "African") are modern labels given by the excavators. No ancient name of any building, painting, colour or pigment is known; Linear A is undeciphered.
- **This is not legal advice.** Topic F (rights) reports what the law texts, ministry decisions and institutional terms opened in this run say. It is research to inform decision D6, not a legal opinion. A Greek lawyer should confirm before any commercial release.

## Section 1: Summary
- **The palette is known by analysis and is larger than "red, yellow, blue, black, white".** Laboratory work since the 1970s identifies lime white, carbon black and manganese-oxide black, red/pink/orange/yellow/brown iron-oxide ochres (haematite, goethite, clay), two blues (synthetic Egyptian blue and a natural blue amphibole now identified as magnesio-riebeckite), shellfish (murex) purple, and greens mixed from yellow ochre and Egyptian blue. Grey comes from riebeckite, from blue mixed with black, or clay with carbon. (#1–#13, #17–#22)
- **Two blues, two looks.** Egyptian blue gives the bright, saturated, "vivid" blue; riebeckite gives grey-blue, dark blue and grey. Painters chose between them by motif, not by cost; in places they laid a thin Egyptian blue layer (25–50 µm) over a coarser riebeckite underlayer to brighten it. (#8–#16)
- **Greens and purples exist but are rare and late-discovered.** Green appears in Xeste 3 (rose branches, rocks, a stone vase's veining, a hunter's shaven head); murex purple is confirmed on Xeste 3 paintings and as a lump of pigment. (#17–#21)
- **Skies are white; seas vary.** In every Theran landscape the sky is the unpainted white plaster, with one exception (a thick Egyptian-blue sky on the Porter's Lodge frieze). On the Miniature Frieze the open sea is grey-blue riebeckite, harbours and river are Egyptian blue, and on the north wall the sea is white plaster hatched with fine blue strokes. (#23–#27)
- **Rocks are bands of red, grey/blue, yellow and black,** but the blue and grey tones are, in the excavators' view, a fairly realistic rendering of the island's dark lava rocks. (#28–#31)
- **Blue on bodies is a convention:** blue/grey heads mean shaven heads; blue in the eye-white marks youth; blue monkeys and birds are most likely grey animals painted with the palette available. (#32–#35)
- **Technique is debated:** lime plaster in layers, string impressions and incised guide lines, preliminary washes; the fresco-versus-secco question is unresolved, and most specialists accept a mixed technique. (#36–#45)
- **Catalogue (Topic A):** Spring Fresco (Delta 2, ground floor, three walls, Athens), Boxing Boys and Antelopes (Beta 1, upper storey, Athens; Boxers 1.95 × 0.94 m), Monkeys (Beta 6, upper floor, Thera museum), West House Room 5 (upper floor, 4 × 4 m: Fishermen, Miniature Frieze) and Room 4, Xeste 3 on three storeys, House of the Ladies (blue papyrus), Xeste 4 (staircase procession), Porter's Lodge. (#46–#66)
- **Pottery of the last phase:** 10–15% imported, mostly Cretan LM IA (spirals, foliate bands, rosettes); local nippled ewers, beak-spouted jugs and piriform jars; Theran swallow jugs were exported. Stone vessels could not be verified. (#67–#74)
- **Rights (Topic F), in one line:** a faithful image of a State-owned antiquity disseminated *for profit* needs a Ministry licence and a fee (Law 4858/2021, Art. 46 §4B; MD 436630/2023); a depiction that goes beyond the object's existing appearance by additions, scientific interpretation, artistic creation or imagination needs prior permission only in special cases (§4D(b), §4A); modern photographs carry their own copyright or (on Wikimedia) a PD-Art claim that Greek heritage law overrides for commercial use. (#75–#90)
- **No measured colour values (Munsell, CIELAB, reflectance) for the Akrotiri paintings were found.** The only Munsell values found are for ochres on Anafi, a putative but unproven source (#91). Pixel values taken from modern museum photographs are not usable (see Section 6, note).

## Section 4: Assumptions checked
- **T6-1 (mostly true fresco):** **could not verify; the evidence points to a debated mixed technique.** Asimenos (1978) argued for secco (#38); the excavation's blue specialists declined to decide (#37); the Building Beta specialist allows an organic binder or lime water (#42); most scholars accept a mixed technique (#40); only an unsourced popular page says fresco (#45).
- **T6-2 (ochres, Egyptian blue and other blues, white, black; few or no greens):** **partly confirmed.** The core palette is confirmed by analysis (#1–#8). "No greens" is contradicted: greens mixed from yellow ochre and Egyptian blue exist in Xeste 3 (#17–#19), as do murex purple (#20, #21), pinks, greys and a manganese black (#4–#6). Greens are rare.
- **T6-3 (painted rooms mostly upper floors):** **partly confirmed.** Many key paintings are upstairs: Beta 1 and Beta 6 (#48), West House Room 5 (#54), Xeste 3 upper storeys (#60–#62). But the Spring Fresco room Delta 2 is on the ground floor (#46) and Xeste 3's ground floor is richly painted (#60, #61). No count of painted rooms by floor was found, so "mostly" could not be verified.
- **T6-4 (rocks as bands of strong, varied colours, not real colours):** **partly confirmed.** Rocks are painted in red, grey/blue, yellow ochre and black (#28–#31), but the excavators read the blue and grey as a fairly realistic rendering of black lava rocks (#29), and Hejl and Tippelt see real tafoni rock forms in the Spring Fresco (Section 5). So: strong, partly conventional colours, not wholly unreal.
- **T6-5 (commercial reproduction needs permission; a code re-creation may too):** **partly confirmed.** Commercial dissemination of faithful images of State-owned antiquities (including movable ones in museums) needs a licence and a fee (#76, #78, #82, #86). A depiction that goes beyond the existing appearance by additions, scientific interpretation or artistic creation needs prior permission only in the special §4A cases (#78, #84). INFERENCE: a code re-creation that reconstructs the original colours and fills gaps is closer to the exempt "creative/scientific interpretation" category than to an "image"; a pixel-faithful copy of the paintings as they look today, shown in a paid product, is closer to an "image" and would likely need a licence. Not legal advice; not settled by any source opened.
- **T6-6 (Beta 6 monkeys and West House Miniature Frieze both final phase):** **partly confirmed.** Both were on the walls in the destruction level (#1, #48, #54); the Beta paintings belong to the building's final decorative phase over earlier painted layers (#66). How long before the eruption either was painted is not known; no source opened dates the Miniature Frieze's making within LC I.

## Section 5: Debates
1. **Fresco or secco.** *Secco:* Asimenos (1978), reported by Hejl and Tippelt, lists string impressions, incised sketches on dry plaster, overpainting and flaking (#38). *Mixed:* Morgan (2018) says most scholars recognise a mixed technique, with planning on damp plaster and much of the painting on dry or re-dampened plaster; she notes Brysbaert (2008) as a proponent of buon fresco and Brecoulaki et al. on secco layering (#40). *Undecided:* Vlachopoulos and Sotiropoulou (2013) refuse to decide for lack of binder data (#37). Building-specific: Georma allows an organic binder or lime water (#42).
2. **"Glaucophane" or riebeckite.** The 1970s analyses called the natural blue glaucophane (#1); later SEM work identified magnesio-riebeckite, and glaucophane is grey when ground (#8, #9). Use "riebeckite" (or "blue amphibole"), not glaucophane.
3. **Why two blues.** Profi et al. (1977) suggested Egyptian blue was imported, expensive and scarce, so it was stretched with the local mineral (#1). Vlachopoulos and Sotiropoulou (2013) argue the choice was by motif and desired tone, not cost (#11). Their Xeste 3 evidence (25–27 m² of Egyptian blue on one storey) argues against scarcity there (#62).
4. **Where the pigments came from.** Red and yellow ochres occur in the pre-eruption rocks of the caldera (#93); Hejl and Tippelt propose Anafi, 20 km east, with no archaeological proof (#91); riebeckite was perhaps local or Cycladic (#15). None is proven.
5. **Real or conventional colours.** The excavators read blue/grey rocks as near-realistic for black lava (#29) but blue monkeys and birds as a palette limitation for grey animals (#35); blue heads as shaven heads (#32). Hejl and Tippelt read the Spring Fresco's rocks as a naturalistic Cycladic landscape with tafoni like today's (#99).
6. **What the Beta 6 animals and walls were.** Marinatos read dogs, then calves; Georma reads goats, and separates them from the Monkeys composition; which walls the Monkeys stood on is uncertain (#51). The B1 animals are read as oryx (Marinatos) or gazelle (Trantalidou), reported by Georma.
7. **Absolute date.** The sources give the end of the 17th century BC (Ephorate, #97) or c. 1600/16th century BC (NAM, #46) or 1600–1500 BC (Profi, #1). This belongs to W1/W2.

## Section 6: Game hooks (suggestions, not evidence)
- **A pigment-based palette, not a photo-based one.** Build the colour system from the analysed materials: lime white (plaster ground), carbon black, manganese black, haematite red, goethite yellow, pink (clay-rich ochre), orange, brown, Egyptian blue (vivid), riebeckite (grey-blue), riebeckite + carbon (grey), Egyptian blue + yellow ochre (green), murex purple. Each token carries its claim number. Tie exact RGB/linear values to measured data when lane verification or a later lane finds any; until then, the values are a liberty (§6 of the standard).
- **Use the Anafi Munsell values (#91) as a starting point for ochres only**, converted with the published Munsell renotation data, and record it as a grade-C liberty.
- **Two blues as two materials:** Egyptian blue for bright motifs (dolphins, rivers, garments, spirals) and riebeckite for broad fields (open sea, rocks, reeds). This is cheap to do in a shader and is what the evidence supports (#12, #13, #24).
- **White skies and white sea-floors in painted interiors** (#23, #27); the one blue sky (Porter's Lodge) can be a small discovery.
- **Painted-room layout:** dado band at the foot (#50, #55), main zone, frieze above windows (#55), orange upper zone close to the ceiling-beam colour (#36), schist floors with red joints (#50).
- **Show "shown is not present" in-world:** the monkeys can appear on walls but not in the landscape, with an in-game note that no monkey bones have been found (#52).
- **Note on photographs (not a claim).** The agent sampled colours from the local reference photographs (which stay local and out of git): museum lighting is strongly warm, and sampled "grey" rocks came out tan. Pixel values from such photographs are not usable as colour data. One local file (`Minoan_saffron_goddess.jpg`) combines a modern painted colour reconstruction (top) with the restored fragments (bottom); the top half must not be treated as evidence.
- **Rights-safe workflow (suggestion for D6):** keep photographs out of the repository; re-create motifs from written descriptions and measurements as interpretive reconstructions (gaps filled, original colours restored, credited as interpretation); do not ship a pixel-faithful reproduction of a painting in a paid product without an ODAP licence; credit "Ministry of Culture" and the museum where a motif is named; get Greek legal advice before any sale.
