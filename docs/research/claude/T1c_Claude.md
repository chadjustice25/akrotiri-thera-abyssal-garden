# T1c: five gaps that block Thera's 1615 BCE terrain

This is a short follow-up to `docs/research/claude/T1_Claude.md` and `T1b_Claude.md`. It covers the five gaps listed in `docs/research/verification/T1.md` ("Gaps") and `T1b.md` ("Gaps"). It does not repeat those lanes; where it builds on a row it cites it as T1#n or T1b#n. Every row cites a page or file opened during this run (retrieved 2026-11-18). "Abstract only" or "preview" marks a row that rests on an abstract or a publisher's preview page. Works that could not be opened are listed at the end and grade nothing.

## How grades are applied in this lane
The landscape reading of the grades in T1 ("How grades are applied") is used unchanged. In short: **A** is a physical observation of Thera's pre-eruption ground or of the eruption deposits themselves; **B** is a present-day measurement carried back, a finding on Thera whose reading is debated, or a published secondary summary of A-grade observations; **C** is a model, an analogy, a hypothesis or a regional value carried to Thera. Dataset and licence rows carry "data" or "licence" instead of a grade. A row marked **INFERENCE** is this lane's own arithmetic, not a source's claim. Place names (Akrotiri, Mavro Rachidi, Kokkino Vouno, Mesovouna, Loumaravas, Potamos, Vlychada, Profitis Ilias, Mesa Vouno) are modern. No ancient name is used.

**Two cautions for the builder.**
- Present-day tephra thickness is not the thickness that fell in c. 1600 BCE. Rain has stripped the tuff from steep slopes and piled it on the coastal plains (#11). Where the tuff is gone, today's surface is close to the pre-eruption ground. Where it has piled up, stripping today's thickness removes eruption deposits and later river gravel together.
- Several thickness figures come from teaching field guides and a web summary of a thesis. Their numbers trace back to Bond and Sparks (1976), Pichler and Friedrich (1980) and Heiken and McCoy (1984), none of which could be opened. These rows carry [verify].

## Section 1: Summary

**1. Thickness of the eruption deposits on land: answered in outline (A/B), not mapped (no isopach map was opened).**
- The Minoan tuffs cover Thera, Therasia and Aspronisi, up to about 55 m thick (#1). The thickness depends on where you stand:
  - **On the high basement** (the outer flanks of Profitis Ilias, Mikro Profitis Ilias and Megalo Vouno), erosion has removed the tuff almost entirely (#11). Today's ground there is close to the 1615 BCE ground. Strip about 0 m.
  - **On the west of the Akrotiri peninsula** (Kokkino Vouno, the Loumaravas height above the Agios Nikolaos chapel), there is no ash at all and the pre-eruption lava is bare (#12).
  - **On the caldera rim** the pile is thickest: Phase 3 ignimbrite alone is 40–55 m, on top of up to about 5.5–6 m of Plinian pumice and up to about 12 m of surge beds (#3–#7). Phase 4 is thin there (0.7–2 m) (#8).
  - **On the eastern and southern coastal plains**, Phase 4 ignimbrite fans reach about 40 m (#8, #9). They are capped by 1–3 m of river gravel and sand, found up to 40 m above sea level (#10).
  - **In the Mavro Rachidi valley**, just west of the town, earlier cores show about 1 m of soil, then post-eruption flood deposits, then about 3–4 m of volcanic ash, then about 1 m of black marine sand, then bedrock (#13). The basement is about 25 m down (#17, T1#37).
- The onshore deposit thickness maps, digitised by Karstens et al., hold 1.43 km³ in total (#18). Spread over the islands' 85 km², that is a present-day mean of about 17 m (#19, INFERENCE).
- **Do not confuse the Minoan pumice with an older one.** Druitt's guide gives ">25 m near Fira to <1 m on the Akrotiri peninsula" for **Lower Pumice 2 (c. 172 ka)**, not for the Minoan eruption (#21).

**2. The caldera before the eruption: outline B, depth and channel still open.**
- The abstract of Druitt and Francaviglia (1992) is now in hand (#24, #25):
  - The caldera wall is built of cliffs of different ages. The S, SE and NW cliffs are fresh and Minoan. The N, NE and E walls keep two pre-Minoan cliff generations.
  - A large flooded caldera, formed c. 21 ka, was an excellent harbour.
  - The Minoan collapse was about 25 km³, and 5–8 km³ of ignimbrite ponded inside the caldera.
- Athanassas et al. (2016) put the old caldera over **at least the northern half** of today's caldera (#26, abstract only).
- No source gives the bay's water depth or the width and depth of a channel. Karátson et al. (2020) say only seismic work and drilling can settle the depth (#27). The IODP 398 caldera holes stayed in post-eruption fill, as far as the weekly reports show (#32).
- **Karátson's reconstructed elevation models are not published as data** (#29–#31):
  - The 2018 paper has no data statement, only a small Word supplement.
  - The base grid (Nomikou et al. 2016) is available "on request".
  - The 2020 paper is CC BY-NC-ND, so its figures may not be adapted.
  - The 2018 paper (CC BY 4.0) is the only one whose figure may be digitised with attribution.

**3. Akrotiri's harbour and south coast: new evidence (B/C), harbour still unlocated.**
- The fuller Theodorakopoulou abstract (#15–#17) adds three findings:
  - Seismics show the pre-Minoan surface, which is Cape Riva ignimbrite, rising from the town to the coast south-east of the excavation, up to about 30 m above sea level.
  - The Potamos valley has kept roughly its Minoan shape, with a floor above present sea level, so there was no bay there.
  - The area south of the site is the better candidate for the harbour.
- The University of Ioannina's Kokkino Vouno project drilled two new cores in the Mavro Rachidi valley in September 2025 (#13, #14):
  - The cores lie about 200 m and 130 m from today's shore.
  - Earlier cores there found marine sand beneath the ash, so the valley was sea or beach when the eruption began.
  - Dates are pending.
- Vlachopoulos (2019) argues that the embayment around the Mesovouna hill, ending at Mavro Rachidi, was the town's natural harbour, with Kokkino Vouno as its western breakwater (#34, C). This probably resolves T1#36's "Mesavouno" as the local hill Mesovouna, not the Mesa Vouno massif (#35, this lane's inference, [verify]).

**4. Relative sea level at Santorini: still open; two regional values conflict (C).**
- No Santorini-specific Holocene sea-level or land-movement study was found.
- Druitt's 2015 guide gives the Aegean in c. 1600 BCE at **less than 0.5 m** below present (after Fleming and Webb 1986) (#37). That conflicts with Kolaiti and Mourtzas's **−3.6 ± 0.2 m** for the northern and central Cyclades (T1#51).
- Measured modern ground movement on Santorini is local, on the Kameni islands, and a few mm a year (#38). The Akrotiri peninsula's uplift is ancient, finished by c. 520 ka (#39).
- Keep −3.6 m as a grade-C liberty, with 0 to −0.5 m recorded as the alternative.

**5. Licences and access (data).**
- **Copernicus DEM GLO-30 and GLO-90** ("Full, Free & Open" instances) (#40–#45):
  - Reproduction, distribution, public communication and adaptation are all allowed, free of charge.
  - A derived, compressed height field inside a public MIT-licensed page is allowed. The page must carry three things: the "produced using Copernicus WorldDEM-30 …" notice, the no-liability sentence, and the same obligations passed on to anyone who redistributes it.
  - So the embedded data cannot be offered under MIT terms alone. The page has to say that the height data stays under the Copernicus terms. That is this lane's reading, not legal advice.
  - Tiles: no account is needed, from `s3://copernicus-dem-30m` (the Santorini tile is 3.4 MB) and `s3://copernicus-dem-90m`.
- **EMODnet DTM 2024** (#46–#48):
  - Licence: CC BY 4.0, with a set acknowledgement sentence.
  - Depths are referred to the lowest astronomical tide (LAT), on a grid of about 115 m.
  - Access: no account, through ERDDAP (`bathymetry_dtm_2024`), tested this run.
- **Greek higher-resolution model** (Hellenic Cadastre): no open licence was found. The portals refused automated access. One university project got the data only under a special permit (#50). Treat it as unavailable.
- **FABDEM** (bare earth) is non-commercial, so it is not suitable (#51).
- **Profitis Ilias** (#53–#55):
  - Summit about 565 m (SwissEduc); the GLO-30 surface model's highest cell this run is 560.4 m.
  - The rock is weakly metamorphosed calcitic and dolomitic marble with Upper Triassic megalodon fossils (Lion 2018) (B).

## Section 4: Debates

1. **Sea level at 1615 BCE: −3.6 m or about 0 m?**
   - Kolaiti and Mourtzas (T1#51, #52) put the northern and central Cyclades at −3.6 ± 0.2 m from c. 2000 BC to c. 30 BC, with a tectonic part of 1–1.2 m.
   - Druitt's guide, citing Fleming and Webb (1986), says the Aegean was less than 0.5 m below present 3,600 years ago (#37).
   - Neither is measured on Santorini, and a volcano can move on its own. The south-coast cores (#13, #14) could settle it once their marine sand is dated and levelled.
   - Until then a choice between the two is a liberty.
2. **What is the bedrock in the Mavro Rachidi valley?**
   - Theodorakopoulou et al. give dense andesitic lava at about 25 m (#17).
   - The Kokkino Vouno project's expected sequence ends in ignimbrite (#13).
   - The pre-Minoan surface south-east of the town is Cape Riva ignimbrite by seismics (#15).
   - These need not conflict: ignimbrite can overlie lava. But the "ground" the builder restores is ignimbrite (or soil on it) near the town, not lava.
3. **Where was the harbour?**
   - South of the site (Theodorakopoulou et al., #17).
   - The Mesovouna–Mavro Rachidi embayment west of the town, sheltered by Kokkino Vouno (Vlachopoulos after Doumas, #34).
   - Harbours on both sides of the site (Doumas 2007, T1b#58).
   - The caldera, about 1 km north (Strasser, T1b#7).
   - Potamos is now ruled out by seismics (#16). The 2025 cores (#14) target the second option.
4. **Phase 2 and Phase 3 maxima.** Phase 2 is given as 7, 10 or 12 m and Phase 3 as 40 or 55 m, depending on author and section (#6, #7). The higher numbers are the more recent compilations.
5. **River gravels: 1–3 m (Druitt 2015, #10) or 5–10 m (Karátson et al. 2018, T1#24)?** The two may describe different beds: ordinary capping gravels versus lithic-rich channel fills.
6. **Bay depth: shallow or deep?** Unchanged from T1b Debates 4.
   - The stromatolites point to a few metres of water (T1b#46).
   - The volume budget needs a large, deep depression (T1b#48), and D&F's abstract calls it "large".
   - Karátson et al. (2020) say only seismics and drilling can decide (#27).
   - The IODP 398 caldera holes, as reported weekly, stayed in post-eruption fill (#32). The expedition's site chapters (not opened) may say more.
7. **Profitis Ilias height: 560, 565 or 567 m.**
   - 560.4 m is a 30 m surface-model cell.
   - 565 and 567 m are popular figures with no stated survey.
   - The Hellenic Military Geographical Service trigonometric point would settle it; it was not found.
